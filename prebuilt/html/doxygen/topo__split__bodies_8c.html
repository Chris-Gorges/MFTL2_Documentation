Detailed Description

From triangle soup to bodies: distributed component labelling, the id decision, and the objects themselves.

Author
Christian Gorges
Date
2026-09-25

LABELS ARE THE SMALLEST TRIANGLE KEY IN THE COMPONENT

A marching cubes triangle has a lattice identity, 16 * cell + slot, that every rank holding it computes identically. A component's label is the smallest such key it contains – a pure function of the component, so every holder converges on the same number without anybody electing anything.

Each round does three things: every LOCAL component takes the smallest label of any triangle in it (MFTLLabelConnectedComponents did the local connectivity); every holder of a GHOST triangle tells its owner the label it has; the owner lowers its component to the smallest it hears and answers with the result. A component spanning k rank boundaries needs at most k + 1 rounds, and the loop ends on a global "nothing changed". No message is sized by the body count or the rank count – only by the ghost triangles.

A body can pass through one rank's box twice with the connection elsewhere – a U whose bend is on the next rank. Locally that is two components; globally one. Objects are therefore built per GLOBAL label, not per local component.

IDS: PROVISIONAL, THEN DENSE

A body's vertex and triangle ids must be dense over all its holders. An owner numbers what it owns from a base MFTLEnsembleExscanBodies() gives it – the library's own per-body scan over each body's holders – and a ghost asks its owner for the id of the entity with its lattice key. The closing redistribution then rebuilds the canonical halo from correct ids.
