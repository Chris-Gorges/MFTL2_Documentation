Detailed Description

Indexed-list to half-edge conversion, the icosphere generator, and marching cubes.

Icosahedron subdivision

A geodesic sphere is built by \(r\) rounds of flat 1-to-4 midpoint subdivision of a regular icosahedron, followed by ONE central projection of every vertex onto the sphere. The icosahedron's twelve vertices are the cyclic permutations of \((0, \pm 1, \pm \varphi)\) with \(\varphi = (1+\sqrt5)/2\).

Counts after \(r\) refinements follow from Euler's formula \(V - E + F = 2\) with \(3F = 2E\):

\[ F_r = 20 \cdot 4^{\,r}, \qquad E_r = 30 \cdot 4^{\,r}, \qquad V_r = 10 \cdot 4^{\,r} + 2 \]

Shapes stay nearly equilateral at every level (mean-ratio quality at least 0.974, mean 0.997), while areas grow towards the icosahedron's face centres by up to a factor of about 2. The enclosed volume converges to \(\tfrac43\pi R^3\) at second order in the edge length.

Marching cubes

The 256-case table is generated from one rule applied to each cell face, so the surface is crack-free and consistently oriented by construction, and every vertex is named by the lattice edge it lies on, so that ranks extracting neighbouring cells produce identical shared vertices without communicating.

Note
Face normals point outward, everywhere. The face table is wound counter-clockwise as seen from outside, and the 1-to-4 subdivision emits every sub-triangle in its parent's rotational order. This is what makes the enclosed volume positive under the divergence theorem and fixes the sign of surface tension and of the indicator function.
