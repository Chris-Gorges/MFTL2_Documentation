Detailed Description

Positions, seam-consistent edge vectors and vertex normals.

The minimum image convention

On a periodic axis the domain is a torus, so the difference of two stored coordinates is not the vector between them. The shortest representative is

\[ \Delta_k = (p_2 - p_1)_k - L_k \,\mathrm{round}\!\left(\frac{(p_2-p_1)_k}{L_k}\right) \]

evaluated with nearbyint, which is one multiply, one round and one fused multiply-subtract per axis. MFTL_Domain::inv_domain_size is precomputed so the division never appears in a hot loop.

Every geometric query in this module applies it. A triangle that straddles a seam has corners on both sides of the box, and a centroid built from the raw coordinates would land in the middle of the domain.

Triangle area and normal

With \(\mathbf{e}_1 = \mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_0\) and \(\mathbf{e}_2 = \mathbf{p}_2 - \mathbf{p}_0\) both taken under the minimum image convention,

\[ \mathbf{n} = \mathbf{e}_1 \times \mathbf{e}_2, \qquad A = \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert \mathbf{n} \rVert, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{\mathbf{n}}{\lVert \mathbf{n} \rVert} \]

so one square root serves both the area and the normalisation.

Warning
A normal is always a unit vector, at every scale. Every kernel in MFTL computes \(m = \lVert\mathbf{n}\rVert\) unclamped and then \(\mathrm{inv} = (m > 0) \,?\, 1/m : 0\). Clamping \(\lVert\mathbf{n}\rVert^2\) before the square root floors the magnitude at \(10^{-6}\); clamping the divisor merely moves the floor. A short normal is the most dangerous shape of wrong available here: it points the right way, so every sign test, winding check and picture still passes, while it silently scales surface tension and every other quantity built from it.

Where the seam correction happens

In registers, per triangle, every time. MFTLComputeTriangleGeometry() dispatches between a compact-index path (MFTL_DERIVED_TOPOLOGY set) and the construction-time half-edge fallback; both gather the three authoritative positions out of verts.pos and apply the minimum image to the two edge vectors, and the two paths are bit-identical.

Caching those vectors per triangle (72 B) made isolated consumer kernels faster but lost in the timestep, which moves every owned vertex and so invalidates the cache before anything reads it (docs/analysis.md §1.2 in the library).

Vertex normals

\[ \mathbf{N}_v = \frac{\sum_{t \ni v} w_t\, \hat{\mathbf{n}}_t} {\left\lVert \sum_{t \ni v} w_t\, \hat{\mathbf{n}}_t \right\rVert}, \qquad w_t = A_t \ \text{(area)} \quad\text{or}\quad w_t = \alpha_{t,v} \ \text{(angle)}, \]

accumulated as a scatter over triangles. Area weighting is parallel to the volume gradient; angle weighting (Thürmer–Wüthrich) makes the pseudo-normal sign test of a signed distance exact (Bærentzen–Aanæs). An owned vertex gets the exact answer iff every triangle incident to it is present locally — a vertex-complete halo. That is what buys the pass zero communication; see Distributed-memory communication.
