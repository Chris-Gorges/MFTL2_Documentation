Detailed Description

Removing dead entities, and re-densifying global ids.

Author
Christian Gorges
Date
2026-08-15

WHY A COLLAPSE CANNOT FREE ANYTHING, AND WHAT FOLLOWS

A triangle's three half-edges ARE slots 3t..3t+2 of the triangle pool. There is no half-edge free list because there is no half-edge allocation, so an operator that removes a triangle cannot hand anything back – it can only mark it. Compaction is therefore not an optimisation here, it is the deallocator.

Two properties of this library make the pass simpler than a general compaction:

HALF-EDGES MOVE AS TRIPLES. he[3*t_new + k] = he[3*t_old + k], so the implicit indexing invariant survives by construction and so do the implicit half-edge global ids (3 * triangle gid + k) – neither needs a fix-up pass. Compacting half-edges as an independent array with a map of their own is what forces next_idx and triangle_idx to be remapped as well.

ONE ALLOCATION PER POOL. Compacting a pool is a loop over its sub-arrays, and a sub-array that someone forgot cannot exist – it would have to have been allocated separately, and nothing outside src/object/object.c can do that.

out_he_idx IS RE-DERIVED, NOT REMAPPED

Remapping it would be one array lookup per vertex and would faithfully preserve whatever each operator left behind, INCLUDING an index the operator failed to repair. Re-deriving it from the surviving half-edges is the same single-pass rule MFTLBuildTopologyFromIndexList uses, costs O(H) rather than O(V), and cannot be stale. It gives up nothing: the fan walkers rewind to the start of an open fan themselves (MFTLVertexFanStart), so which outgoing half-edge a vertex names has never mattered.

COMPACTION IS ALSO WHERE THE OWNERSHIP PREFIX IS RE-ESTABLISHED

An operator appends, and an append lands at the end of the pool – past the ghosts. So during a parallel remeshing sweep the pool is 

[ owned ...... | ghosts ...... | entities created this sweep ...... ]
               ^ n_local

which violates invariant 2 of mftl_types.h (ownership is a prefix) for exactly as long as the sweep runs. This function is what ends that window, and it is the natural place: it is already a full permutation of both pools with a full index remap behind it, so restoring the prefix costs one more block move and one more term in the map – not a second pass.

The permutation is not a forward compaction any more, because a ghost may have to move RIGHT to make room for the entities created after it. It is a forward compaction FOLLOWED BY a swap of the two trailing blocks, and that is the whole of it: the forward compaction preserves relative order, so the survivors come out as [owned][ghosts][created] and the prefix is restored by exchanging the last two. A general permutation would need cycle-following or a full copy of both pools; two adjacent blocks need one buffer the size of the smaller of them and two memmoves.

On a serial object there are no ghosts, an append increments n_local with the pool, and both trailing blocks are empty.

◆ MFTLRemeshCompactObject()

int MFTLRemeshCompactObject ( MFTL_Object * obj,
MFTL_RemeshStats * stats )

Removes every MFTL_FLAG_DEAD entity and every orphaned vertex, and restores the ownership prefix.

A forward compaction of both pools (half-edges move as triples with their triangle, which preserves implicit indexing and the implicit half-edge ids) followed, on a partitioned object, by a swap of the two trailing blocks: the sweeps append past the ghosts, so the survivors come out as [owned | ghosts | created] and the swap makes the owned entities a prefix again. he.vertex_idx and he.twin_idx are remapped, out_he_idx is re-derived from the surviving half-edges, the derived arrays are rebuilt, and the cached geometry is permuted along with its entities.

Parameters
objThe mesh, compacted in place.
[out]statsMay be MFTL_NULL. n_vertices_removed and n_orphans_removed are accumulated into it.
Returns
The number of entities removed (vertices plus triangles).
Rank-local:
Performs no MPI. Each rank operates on its own partition.
