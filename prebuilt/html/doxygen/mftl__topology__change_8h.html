Detailed Description

Topology changes – breakup and coalescence – by distributed signed-distance rasterisation and marching cubes, on the partition as it stands.

Author
Christian Gorges
Date
2026-09-25

WHAT HAPPENS

  1. DETECT. Pairs of triangles with opposing normals closer than a threshold, within one body and between two (MFTLEnsembleIntersectObjects with self-proximity). A pair whose gap lies INSIDE the liquid is a thread or sheet about to BREAK; one whose gap lies outside is a film about to RUPTURE and let two surfaces COALESCE.
  2. AGREE. Every rank learns every event, and the bodies they touch are grouped into CLUSTERS by union-find: bodies joined by a coalescence event are reconstructed together.
  3. RECONSTRUCT each cluster's bodies WHOLE from their signed distance field on a global, wall-aligned lattice, with a smooth local bias at every event that opens the thread or closes the film – so the outcome is decided by the event, not by whether a lattice node happens to fall in a gap. Marching cubes then triangulates the zero level set.
  4. SEPARATE the result into connected components, one body each.
  5. REDISTRIBUTE to the canonical partition, and optionally remesh.

NOTHING MIGRATES

Every step runs on the partition the caller has. Each rank rasterises its OWNED triangles onto a window around its own box and the neighbours merge the overlap by a commutative rule, so every rank ends up with the exact field on its box. Each rank then runs marching cubes on its own cells plus one layer of its neighbours', which builds the ghost halo directly. A surface vertex is named by the lattice EDGE it lies on and produced from bit-identical values on every rank that produces it, so the ranks' pieces fit together with no stitching and no negotiation. Components are labelled by propagating the smallest triangle key across ghost triangles until nothing changes.

The reconstructed surface is therefore independent of the process count up to the last bit of the pseudo-normal sums (see test_topology_change.c).

WHAT IT COSTS, AND WHAT IT CHANGES

A reconstructed body is re-triangulated at the lattice spacing EVERYWHERE, not only at the event: vertices are new, ids are new, and its surface moves by the marching cubes error, O(dx^2) times the curvature, with a local volume change of order bias x area at each event. Bodies not in a cluster are not touched at all. A rim on a domain wall is reproduced ON the wall, as the surface's intersection with the wall plane at lattice resolution; a rim anywhere else cannot be represented by a level set, so a cluster containing one is refused rather than corrupted.

Collective throughout: every rank calls with the same options.

What kind of thin feature an event is.

Enumerator
MFTL_TOPOLOGY_BREAKUP 

The gap between the two triangles is INSIDE the body: a thread or a sheet of liquid thinner than delta_breakup. It is cut open.

MFTL_TOPOLOGY_COALESCENCE 

The gap is OUTSIDE: a film of the surrounding phase thinner than delta_coalescence, between two bodies or two parts of one. It is closed.

void MFTLTopologyLattice ( const MFTL_Domain * domain,
double dx,
double spacing[3],
int cells[3] )

The reconstruction lattice for a domain and a target spacing.

Axis k has cells[k] = cuts[k] * max(1, round(L_k / (cuts[k] * dx))) cells of spacing[k] = L_k / cells[k], anchored at global_min[k]. Two properties follow and the whole reconstruction rests on them: a node plane lies on every domain wall, so a surface meeting a wall is cut exactly there, and a node plane lies on every rank's cut plane, so every lattice cell belongs to exactly one rank.

Parameters
domainThe domain.
dxTarget spacing, > 0.
[out]spacingActual spacing per axis.
[out]cellsCells per axis.
Rank-local:
Performs no MPI. Each rank operates on its own partition. Pure function of the domain.

The returned list is IDENTICAL on every rank: gathered, sorted and with duplicates removed (a gap seen from both sides, or by two ranks, is one event; event points in one cell of a quarter of max(smaller threshold, dx) are merged). Pairs sharing a vertex are never events.

Reconstructs the listed bodies from their signed distance field, biased at the listed events, and splits the result into bodies.

The core of MFTLEnsembleApplyTopologyChanges(), exposed because it is useful on its own – re-gridding a body, or reconstructing one with no events at all to test the pipeline – and because it is where every distributed property of the module lives.

CLUSTERS are the connected components of the listed bodies under the events between two of them – of either kind: a coalescence joins two bodies, and a liquid film between a drop and a bubble inside it is a breakup event between two bodies that bound one liquid from opposite sides. Each cluster's bodies are rasterised together, so the field is the signed distance to the UNION of their surfaces, signed by the nearest one – which is correct for nested bodies as well, where a min over per-body fields would not be. Ids: in each cluster the largest resulting component keeps the id of the largest body that went in, the second the second, and so on; components beyond that get new ids, and bodies beyond that are retired. So the jet a drop pinches off stays the jet, and the pool a drop falls into stays the pool.

Closes with MFTLRedistribute() to the canonical partition, current triangle geometry and global counts – and then, when opts->remesh is set, one MFTLRemeshEnsemble() over the whole ensemble.

Parameters
ensThe ensemble. After the call every MFTL_Object pointer of a listed body is invalid – use MFTLEnsembleFindLocalObject() – and so are all local indices, as after any redistribution.
bodies[n_bodies] body ids to reconstruct, identical on every rank.
n_bodiesTheir number.
events[n_events] events, identical on every rank; each biases the field of the cluster its bodies are in. May be MFTL_NULL.
n_eventsTheir number.
optsThe options.
[out]statsOptional.
Returns
MFTL_SUCCESS, or MFTL_ERROR (on every rank).
Collective:
Must be called by every rank of the ensemble's communicator.

◆ MFTLEnsembleApplyTopologyChanges()

int MFTLEnsembleApplyTopologyChanges ( MFTL_Ensemble * ens,
const MFTL_TopologyOptions * opts,
MFTL_TopologyStats * stats )

Detects, reconstructs and remeshes: one topology-change step.

MFTLTopologyDetectEvents(), then MFTLEnsembleReconstructBodies() on every body an event touches (which remeshes when opts->remesh is set). A call that finds no event changes nothing at all: no redistribution, no remesh, every pool untouched.

Call it where a remeshing pass would go: after the vertices moved and the ghost positions were synchronised, before the geometry a solver reads.

Parameters
ensThe ensemble. Same pointer rules as MFTLEnsembleReconstructBodies().
optsThe options.
[out]statsOptional.
Returns
MFTL_SUCCESS, or MFTL_ERROR (on every rank).
Collective:
Must be called by every rank of the ensemble's communicator.
