Detailed Description

Split, collapse, flip, smooth — serial operators plus two distributed schedules around them.

The algorithm

Botsch & Kobbelt's incremental isotropic remesher. One iteration, against a target edge length \(L\):

  1. Split every edge longer than \(\tfrac{4}{3}L\)
  2. Collapse every edge shorter than \(\tfrac{4}{5}L\), unless a resulting edge would be longer than \(\tfrac43 L\)
  3. Flip every edge whose flip strictly lowers the squared valence deviation
  4. Smooth tangentially (with feature detection before 1 and before 4)

Splitting an edge longer than \(\tfrac43 L\) gives halves longer than \(\tfrac23 L\), no further from \(L\) than the original. The band alone does not exclude split/collapse oscillation (an edge between \(\tfrac43 L\) and \(\tfrac85 L\) splits into collapsible halves); the collapse's stretch clause does.

Valence optimisation

A flip is accepted when it lowers

\[ E = \sum_{v \in \{a,b,c,d\}} \big(\deg(v) - \deg^{*}(v)\big)^{2}, \qquad \deg^{*} = \begin{cases} 6 & \text{interior} \\ 4 & \text{boundary} \end{cases} \]

over the four vertices of the quad. \(\deg^{*} = 6\) is the valence of a regular triangulation of the plane.

Smoothing is a projection

The feature class of a vertex chooses the subspace the Laplacian displacement is projected onto:

Class Subspace Update
free 2D tangent plane \(\mathbf{d} \leftarrow (\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{n}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\!\top})\,\mathbf{d}\)
ridge 1D curve tangent \(\mathbf{d} \leftarrow (\hat{\mathbf{t}}\hat{\mathbf{t}}^{\!\top})\,\mathbf{d}\)
corner 0D \(\mathbf{d} \leftarrow \mathbf{0}\)

Stated that way the three cases are one idea. Moving a ridge vertex to the midpoint of its two feature neighbours — the full displacement, no projection — is the 2D shrinkage problem reintroduced on a 1D curve: a regular 24-gon ridge collapses to 0.84 of its radius in 20 sweeps.

Two distributed strategies, chosen by one ratio

An edge whose endpoints are owned by different ranks cannot be collapsed by either of them: each holds half of the one-ring the link condition is a statement about. Both strategies remove that situation rather than negotiating it, and MFTLRemeshChooseStrategy() picks between them from reduced data.

They share no code past MFTLRemeshIterate(), which is what makes them each other's cross-check: agreeing on the answer is evidence about the ANSWER rather than about one implementation.

The covering condition

An owned entity is FROZEN exactly when some other rank holds a copy of it, which MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen() derives locally from the ownership prefix — no distance test and no message. Write \(\delta\) for the half-width of the band of frozen entities either side of a cut plane and \(h_k\) for the rank box width. An entity frozen in one placement is at distance \(\ge h_k/2 - \delta\) from the planes of the half-shifted placement, so it is interior there iff

\[ \frac{h_k}{2} - \delta > \delta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h_k > 4\delta . \]

Under that condition each axis has at most ONE bad shift value, so an entity's conflict is a vector in \(\{0,1,\varnothing\}^3\) and a placement works iff it differs from that vector on every conflicting axis. Hence: two placements cover every one-axis (slab) conflict, a strength-2 orthogonal array of four covers every two-axis (tube) conflict, and all eight are needed — and suffice — for a three-axis (corner) conflict. That is MFTL_REMESH_PHASES, ordered so that its prefixes are the useful covering sets.

When \(h_k > 4\delta\) fails, parallel_coarsen merges \(g_k = \lceil 4\delta/h_k \rceil\) boxes per axis through MFTL_OwnerMap — a coarser decomposition is just a different owner function, so no second communicator is involved.

Note
All of the module's MPI is in one file, src/remeshing/remesh_comm.c, enforced at configure time by a CMake source check. The operators, the smoother, the compaction and both distributed drivers contain none.
