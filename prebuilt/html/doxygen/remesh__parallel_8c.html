Detailed Description

Distributed remeshing: the shift/freeze phase schedule.

Author
Christian Gorges
Date
2026-08-17

THE PROBLEM, AND THE ONE IDEA THAT REMOVES IT

An edge whose two endpoints are owned by different ranks cannot be collapsed by either of them: each holds half of the one-ring that the link condition is a statement about, and each would have to rewrite records the other owns. The obvious answer is a protocol – lock the edge, ask the neighbour, agree, act – and it is the wrong one. It puts a round trip inside the innermost loop of a remesher, and it makes the RESULT depend on message arrival order, so the same mesh remeshes differently on the same hardware twice in a row.

The idea that removes the problem instead of managing it: 

DON'T MOVE THE MESH TO SUIT THE CUT PLANES. MOVE THE CUT PLANES.

Run the untouched serial kernel on the INTERIOR of each rank's share, where the whole modified neighbourhood is exclusively that rank's; refuse every operation whose footprint reaches the partition boundary; then MOVE THE PLANES BY HALF A BOX and run it again. What was on the boundary is now in the middle of somebody's share, and gets its turn. Nothing is negotiated because nothing overlaps, and the answer does not depend on timing because no two ranks ever touch the same entity.

THE GEOMETRIC GUARANTEE, AND WHAT IT COSTS

Let delta be the geometric half-width of the frozen band and h the rank box width. A point frozen in the canonical placement is at distance <= delta from a plane, hence at distance >= h/2 - delta from the nearest plane of the half-shifted placement. It is interior there iff 

h/2 - delta > delta   <=>   delta < h/4   <=>   h > 4 delta

which is the hard precondition of the whole scheme, checked in MFTLRemeshEnsembleParallel and reported rather than assumed.

That is the PER-AXIS statement. In 3D an entity can sit near planes of one, two or three axes at once, and the diagonal shift (1,1,1) only rescues the first case:

SLAB near 1 axis measure O(delta/h) 2 phases: (0,0,0), (1,1,1) TUBE near 2 axes measure O((delta/h)^2) 4 phases: {000,011,101,110} CORNER near 3 axes measure O((delta/h)^3) 8 phases: all of {0,h/2}^3

Under h > 4 delta an entity is near the planes of at most one of the two placements per axis. One near the x planes of placement 0 and the y planes of placement 1 is rescued by neither (0,0,0) nor (1,1,1) – it needs s_x = 1, s_y = 0 – and the even-parity set contains every such pair on every pair of axes. The table is ordered so that the placements that matter most run first: two handle all but O((delta/h)^2) of the mesh, and the tail rows typically find a handful of entities each.

"FROZEN" IS DERIVED, NOT COMMUNICATED, AND IT IS EXACT

The frozen set is defined by an equivalence rather than by a distance: 

an owned entity is frozen  <=>  some other rank holds a copy of it

and this rank can evaluate the right-hand side ALONE, because it is the same rule the redistribution used when it decided what to send. An owned triangle is sent to the owner of each of its three vertices and of each of its three edge neighbours (src/communication/redistribute.c, collect_destinations), so 

triangle t is replicated  <=>  a corner of t is a ghost
                               OR an edge neighbour of t is a ghost

and with ownership as a prefix, "is a ghost" is index >= n_local. The classification is therefore two streaming reads (tris.verts, tris.neighbors, 12 bytes each per triangle) and six integer compares, with no distance test, no tolerance, and no message. See MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen.

Being exact rather than conservative is what makes the DILATION the plan called for unnecessary, and the argument is short enough to state in full:

  1. A vertex is frozen if ANY incident triangle is frozen. So an unfrozen vertex has every incident triangle unfrozen – owned, and replicated nowhere.
  2. An unfrozen triangle has no ghost corner, so all three of its corners are OWNED. Combined with 1: every vertex an operator reads while judging an operation on unfrozen entities is owned by this rank.
  3. The redistribution halo is VERTEX-COMPLETE (inv_halo_is_vertex_complete), so every triangle incident to an owned vertex is present locally. With 2, every fan walk an approval predicate performs – the link condition, the wing valences, the stretch bound, the fold-over test – traverses a COMPLETE fan and gets the same answer it would get on the whole mesh.

The converse direction is what makes the phase safe rather than merely self-consistent, and it is worth stating separately because it is what buys the missing communication:

  1. Every ghost in this rank's pool is frozen ON ITS OWNER. A ghost triangle is here because this rank owns one of its corners or one of its edge neighbours – which, seen from the owner, IS the definition of replicated. So no neighbour may rewrite it, delete it, or move any of its corners.

Therefore this rank's ghost copies are still EXACT when the sweeps finish, and the phase needs no ghost position sync before the next migration – the one per-phase collective the design might have been expected to pay.

GLOBAL IDS: SPARSE DURING THE PASS, DENSE AT THE END

Ids must stay unique across ranks and, at the end, dense: redistribution and the ghost sync size their arrival tables from next_*_gid, which rules out a rank-encoded 64-bit id. So ids stay 32-bit, in two steps:

PER PHASE the operators draw ids from the object's own counter (locally unique, globally ambiguous). Entities created in the phase are exactly those with global_id >= the watermark recorded before the sweeps, and a per-body exclusive prefix sum of their counts over the body's holders (MFTLEnsembleExscanBodies()) moves each rank's block to a disjoint range. Unique, not yet dense.

AT THE END a prefix sum of the OWNED counts renumbers every owned entity to base + slot, dense by construction. Ghost vertices learn their new ids from MFTLRemeshSyncGhostIds(); ghost triangles need not, because the closing redistribution re-receives them.

The order of those steps is forced; see redensify_global_ids().

◆ MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen()

void MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen ( MFTL_Object * obj)

Flags MFTL_FLAG_FROZEN on every entity this rank may not modify.

An owned entity is frozen exactly when another rank holds a copy of it. Redistribution sends a triangle to the owner of each of its corners and edge neighbours, so an owned triangle is replicated iff corner >= verts.n_local || neighbour >= tris.n_local – read from tris.verts and tris.neighbors, no distance, no message. Frozen are: every ghost, every replicated owned triangle, and every vertex of a frozen triangle; the flag is cleared elsewhere. Hence an unfrozen vertex has only owned, unreplicated incident triangles, and (with the vertex-complete halo) its whole fan is local. On a ghost-free object nothing is frozen. Stamps the partition dirty, since flags travel with kept ghosts.

const MFTL_RemeshParameters * params,
int * hidden_long,
int * hidden_short )

The convergence certificate: out-of-band edges that THIS placement of the cut planes hides from every rank, split by the operator that would fix them.

Needs no fan walk, so a rank's partial view of a neighbour's one-ring cannot give it a wrong answer (unlike counting "approved, then refused by the gate"; see sweep_split in remesh_driver.c). An edge is hidden when its operator's gate refuses it: a split is gated by the two incident TRIANGLES (freeze_blocks_pair), a collapse by the two endpoint VERTICES (freeze_blocks_collapse). The vertex-frozen set strictly contains the triangle-frozen one, so testing long edges with the vertex rule would over-report and suppress the fast exit.

hidden_long is EXACT. If it is zero and no rank performed an operation, every long edge had both incident triangles unfrozen on the rank counting it, hence owned (every ghost is frozen); the split sweep reached it and MFTLSplitApproved() refuses only DEAD, VISITED or non-canonical edges, both flags being clear at measurement time – so it would have been split, contradiction. hidden_short is not exact: a collapse can be refused on merit in both directions (link condition, wing valence, stretch, feature rules), and such an edge stays hidden forever – the false alarm the silent-streak exit absorbs. An edge no placement ever offers (the \(h \le 4\delta\) failure) is counted in every phase.

Called BEFORE the sweeps: in the converged regime, the only one whose value is read, the sweeps change nothing; afterwards, new entities would carry provisional ids that the cross-rank attribution rule cannot compare.

── THE SCOPE IS AUTO, AND IT IS NOT A DETAIL ─────────────────────────────

MFTL_BODY_REDUCE_SCOPE_AUTO lets MFTLEnsembleExscanBodies() take the route the partition actually permits: the body's HOLDERS over the 26-neighbour graph when every holder set fits inside one neighbourhood, and comm_world when it does not – which is exactly what a COARSENED placement makes normal, because a coarse representative is group fine cells away and no local test would call it a neighbour. ◆ redensify_global_ids()

int redensify_global_ids ( MFTL_Ensemble * ens,
gid_scratch * g )
static

Makes the ids of both classes a dense permutation of [0, N) again.

── The three steps are ordered, and every ordering constraint is silent ───

  1. REDUCE first. The prefix sum of the owned counts is what "base + slot" means, and the ghost exchange in step 2 answers with it.
  2. EXCHANGE before overwriting. The request is keyed on the OLD id, so the owner must still be holding old ids when it answers. Renumbering first would make every request unanswerable – and the reply path treats an unanswerable request as a skip, so the ghosts would quietly keep stale ids instead of failing.
  3. OVERWRITE last, by SLOT. Any other rule would need a second table on the owner to answer step 2; numbering by slot makes the answer arithmetic.

Ghost TRIANGLES are deliberately not exchanged. Redistribution keeps only owned triangles and re-receives every ghost, so a ghost triangle's id is replaced wholesale by the caller's closing migration – which is also why that migration has to come AFTER this function and not before it.

Parameters
ensThe ensemble.
gThe scratch handle.
Returns
MFTL_SUCCESS, or MFTL_ERROR if the ghost id exchange failed.

The band this partition ACTUALLY has, and an upper bound on it.

══ WHY THE ESTIMATE IS NOT GOOD ENOUGH, WITH NUMBERS ══════════════════════

frozen_band_width is 3 * max_edge_length, derived from the reach of the freeze rule: one triangle to the plane, one more to a triangle that reaches it, one more because a vertex is frozen when ANY incident triangle is. It is an upper bound and it is a loose one. Measured on a partitioned sphere in [-1,1]^3 across a sweep of target lengths, the band that actually occurs is 

0.84 * max_edge_length

– about ONE edge, not three, and consistently so from target 0.06 to 0.50. The consequence is not academic: h > 4*delta computed from the estimate refuses configurations that work perfectly. At a target of 0.12 on that fixture the estimate says 4*delta = 1.92 against h = 1.0, i.e. "this cannot work", and the pass converges with nothing hidden at every rank count. A precondition that cries wolf by a factor of three is a precondition nobody reads.

So the band is measured. Two numbers, because they answer different questions:

out[0] band_measured – max over this rank's OWNED FROZEN vertices of the distance to the nearest cut plane. What the band IS, right now.

out[1] the longest owned edge, which times MFTL_REMESH_BAND_EDGES is a measured upper bound for the band later in the pass. Note it can legitimately exceed params->max_edge_length: a collapse can lengthen an edge past the band, and the split sweep's snapshot bound defers the correction by one iteration.

── The trap in out[0], which the obvious spelling walks into ──────────────

The distance has to be the MINIMUM over axes, then the maximum over vertices. A vertex frozen because of a y plane sits about h/2 from every x plane, so a maximum over axes reports h/2 on the first sample and the measurement is worthless. Axes with cuts < 2 are skipped for the same reason they are skipped in MFTLDomainGetHMin: they have no interior plane to be near.

LOCAL, no MPI – the caller reduces both with one MFTLRemeshAgreeDoubleMax.

objThe mesh.
domThe MPI/physical world.
shiftPer-axis cut-plane offset of the current phase.

── Why TRIANGLES and not vertices ─────────────────────────────────────────

Because the quantity being estimated is the share of a MIGRATION that buys nothing, and the migration's payload is dominated by triangles: a closed mesh has twice as many of them as vertices, and each carries three half-edges. ◆ band_for_frozen_target()

double band_for_frozen_target ( const MFTL_Domain * dom,
double delta,
double f_target )
static

The band that would hold the frozen share at or under f_target.

── One formula, two constants ─────────────────────────────────────────────

The correctness rule is g[k] = ceil(4*delta / h_k) and the efficiency rule is g[k] = ceil(2*delta / ((1-u) * h_k)), so they differ only by a constant and the second can be expressed as the first applied to an INFLATED BAND. That is why this returns a band rather than a factor: it lets both triggers go through the one published MFTLRemeshCoarseningFactors, which is tested, clamps to cuts[k], and skips uncut axes – three behaviours a second implementation would have to reproduce and would eventually stop reproducing.

── Where u comes from ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A box loses 2*delta per cut axis, so the unfrozen share is the product over cut axes of (1 - 2*delta/h_k). Requiring each axis to contribute at least u = (1 - f_target)^(1/n_cut) makes the product at least 1 - f_target, and is the only split of the requirement across axes that does not privilege one. Solving 1 - 2*delta/(g*h) >= u for g gives the rule above.

Check it against the degenerate case: with ONE cut axis, u = 1 - f_target, so the band inflates by 1/(2*f_target) and at a third that is h >= 6*delta – which is f = 2*delta/h <= 1/3 read straight off, with no model in between.

Reports, ONCE per process, that the rank boxes are too small for the shift schedule.

── Why this warns and does not assert ─────────────────────────────────────

Because h <= 4*delta makes the scheme DEGRADE, not break. The mesh that comes out is structurally perfect; it simply has bands along the cut planes that no placement ever offered to anyone, so they keep whatever triangles they had. MFTL_RemeshStats.converged says so, and the caller can act on it.

An assertion would abort a running simulation over a mesh-QUALITY shortfall, which is the wrong trade at 3 a.m. on a cluster. It is also wrong for a duller reason that only showed up under test: freeze_dilation_rings is a documented, supported parameter that WIDENS delta, so a configuration the API invites the caller to try would abort in Debug and run in Release. A knob whose whole purpose is to be conservative must not be the thing that kills the process.

The one-shot latch is not cosmetic either. A solver remeshes every N timesteps, so a per-call warning is thousands of identical lines – and the reliable effect of that is that nobody reads any of them.

Remeshes a distributed ensemble by shifting the cut planes (SPANNING).

Normally reached through MFTLRemeshEnsemble(); public so the schedule can be run at nproc == 1 against the serial answer. 

[optional pre-pass: measure band and frozen share, choose coarsening]
per placement: migrate (skipped if identity) -> MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen
