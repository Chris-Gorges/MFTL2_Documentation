Detailed Description

The field and the surface: rasterisation, the overlap exchange, the bias, and marching cubes on this rank's cells.

Author
Christian Gorges
Date
2026-09-25

WHY EACH RANK RASTERISES ONLY WHAT IT OWNS

The value at a lattice node is the minimum over EVERY triangle within the cutoff, and those triangles may be owned by any of up to eight ranks around the node. Two ways to get it:

PULL the triangles: grow a spatial halo of every neighbour's triangles near this box and rasterise everything locally. Triangles near a corner are shipped to seven ranks and rasterised by eight.

PUSH the values: each rank rasterises the triangles it OWNS – each exactly once in the job – onto a padded window, and sends every node another rank needs. MFTLSdfMerge() is commutative and associative, so the merged value is the same whatever order the contributions arrive in, and bit-identical to a serial rasterisation of the whole mesh.

PUSH is what a CFD code does with a ghost-cell halo, and it moves one double per boundary node instead of a triangle per nearby triangle.

WHICH NODES A RANK NEEDS

Its own box of nodes and one more layer on every side: marching cubes runs on its own cells AND one layer of the neighbours' cells, so that the triangles around every vertex it owns are present – the vertex-complete halo, built by the extraction rather than by a later exchange.
