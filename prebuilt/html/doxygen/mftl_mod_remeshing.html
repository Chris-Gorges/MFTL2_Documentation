The serial algorithm

Botsch & Kobbelt's incremental isotropic remesher (SGP 2004). One iteration of MFTLRemeshIterate:

DETECT → SPLIT (ℓ > ℓmax) → COLLAPSE (ℓ < ℓmin) → COMPACT → FLIP → DETECT → SMOOTH

repeated params.iterations times or until an iteration performs at most min_convergence_ops operations. MFTLRemeshObject wraps it with an assertion that the object holds no ghosts, the id renumbering and a closing geometry pass.

The length band

A split maps \(\ell \mapsto \ell/2\), so an edge longer than \(\ell_{\max}\) gives halves longer than \(\tfrac23 L\): \(|\tfrac43 L - L| = |\tfrac23 L - L|\), i.e. a split never moves an edge further from \(L\). The band does not by itself exclude split/collapse ping-pong — an edge in \((\tfrac43 L, \tfrac85 L)\) splits into halves below \(\ell_{\min}\) — and three mechanisms do:

  1. every sweep bounds its loop by the half-edge count taken before it, so new halves are not re-examined until the next iteration, after smoothing;
  2. MFTLCollapseApproved refuses a collapse that would create an edge longer than \(\ell_{\max}\) (the stretch clause);
  3. smoothing between sweeps pulls the length distribution into the band.
Figure 1. The length band. A split halves an edge, and \(\tfrac43L\) and \(\tfrac23L\) are equally far from \(L\), so a split never moves an edge away from the target. Edges in \((\tfrac43L, \tfrac85L)\) (hatched) split into halves below \(\tfrac45L\), which is why the band alone does not rule out split/collapse ping-pong.

MFTL_REMESH_CUSTOM lets the caller set any band (then MFTLRemeshParamsFinalise).

The sweep order

  • SPLIT before COLLAPSE: splitting cannot fail (no link condition, no fold test), so the collapse sweep sees edges that are genuinely short.
  • COMPACT right after COLLAPSE: flip and smoothing never see a dead entity, so MFTLComputeVertexNormals can run over the whole pool.
  • FLIP before SMOOTH: the valence criterion reads the connectivity just produced; smoothing does not change it.
  • DETECT twice: splits and collapses change which edges exist. A split of a rim edge creates a midpoint on the rim; classified as free, it would slide off the rim, leaving a notch no structural check can see.

Operators under implicit indexing

Half-edge \(3t+k\) is corner \(k\) of triangle \(t\), so an operator cannot allocate a half-edge or patch a next pointer. It rewrites whole triangles and re-links the twins on the rim of its patch:

Operator Allocation Work
FLIP none two triangles rewritten in place, four rim twins re-pointed
SPLIT one triangle per side (with its three half-edges) midpoint vertex appended, 2–4 triangles written
COLLAPSE none, and nothing freed one or two triangles and the origin vertex flagged MFTL_FLAG_DEAD for compaction
Figure 2. The three operators, before (top) and after (bottom). A split appends the midpoint and one triangle per side. A collapse moves \(b\) to \(p_m\), rewrites \(a\)'s fan, and marks \(a\) and the two triangles on the edge dead for compaction. A flip re-cuts a quad only if the valence energy strictly drops, here from 4 to 0.

Half-edge ids, \(3\,t_\mathrm{gid}+k\), belong to the slot and stay valid. The flag word does not: when a triangle is rewritten a logical edge may move to another slot, so every operator captures the rim flags (FEATURE, BOUNDARY) first and places them explicitly. A split's midpoint inherits the edge's classification, so the output is consistent before the next detection.

Approval and execution are separate; the predicates carry the whole correctness argument. SPLIT and FLIP are offered once per undirected edge (the smaller half-edge index); COLLAPSE is offered both directions, because its feature rules are asymmetric. MFTL_FLAG_VISITED, cleared once per iteration, keeps an entity from being operated on twice before the next smoothing.

The collapse predicate

Collapsing \(h = (a\to b)\) retires \(a\), moves \(b\) to \(p_m\) and rewrites every triangle of \(a\)'s fan. With \(N(v)\) the one-ring and \(W = N(a)\cap N(b)\) the wing vertices, in evaluation order:

# Clause Statement Without it
1 wanted \(\lVert e_h\rVert < \ell_{\min}\) —
2 features \(a\) not a corner; \(a\) on a feature curve ⟹ \(b\) too; \(a\) on the boundary ⟹ \(b\) too the curve or rim is dragged off itself
3 curve link not ( \(h\) not a feature edge ∧ \(a, b\) both on feature curves); not ( \(h\) interior ∧ \(a, b\) both on the boundary) a chord collapses: an open patch's rim is serrated, two sharp edges of a cube merge
4 link condition \(\lvert W\rvert = 2\) ( \(1\) at a boundary edge) two sheets welded into a non-manifold vertex
5 merged valence \(3 \le \lvert N(a)\rvert + \lvert N(b)\rvert - \lvert W\rvert - 2 \le\) MFTL_MAX_VALENCE untraversable vertex
6 wing valence \(\lvert N(w)\rvert \ge 4\) for \(w \in W\) a tetrahedron passes clause 4 and dissolves
7 stretch \(\lVert p_m - p_w\rVert \le \ell_{\max}\) for all \(w \in N(a)\cup N(b)\) split/collapse oscillation
8 fold-over \(\cos\angle(\mathbf n_\text{old}, \mathbf n_\text{new}) \ge\) max_normal_deviation_cos for every surviving triangle; degenerate results refused inverted triangles

\(p_m = p_b\) if \(b\) is a corner or \(b\) alone lies on a feature curve or boundary; otherwise the minimum-image midpoint (exact on a straight feature, \(O(h^2)\) off a curved one, acceptable because the edge is short). None of these clauses fails loudly; clauses 3 and 6 are the ones the textbook link condition does not imply (test_collapse_refuses_to_dissolve_a_tetrahedron, test_remesh_of_an_open_patch_keeps_its_rim). The fold test is relative to the triangle's own edges (squared sine of a corner angle against \(10^{-15}\)), so the result is independent of the length unit: test_remeshing_is_independent_of_the_length_unit requires a sphere remeshed at scale \(2^{-20}\) to equal the unit result times \(2^{-20}\) bit for bit. The fold-over guard also bounds a runaway once features are spurious (16 702 triangles against 101 048).

Figure 3. Four clauses of the collapse predicate, none of which fails loudly. (a) An allowed collapse: \(a\) and \(b\) share exactly the two wing vertices. (b) A third common neighbour \(x\) violates the link condition; the collapse would weld two sheets. (c) A tetrahedron passes the link condition, but its wings have valence 3 and it would dissolve. (d) \(a\) and \(b\) both lie on the rim but the edge between them does not: collapsing that chord pinches the rim inward.

Valence optimisation

A flip re-cuts the quad \((a,b,c,d)\), so \(a, b\) lose a neighbour and \(c, d\) gain one. It is accepted only on a strict decrease of

\[ E = \sum_{v \in \{a,b,c,d\}} \big(\deg v - \deg^* v\big)^2, \qquad \deg^* = 6 \text{ interior},\ 4 \text{ boundary} \]

( \(\deg\) counts neighbours; 6 is the regular valence of the plane, 4 of the half-plane). Equality would let two flips alternate forever. Refused for feature and boundary edges, when \(\deg a\) or \(\deg b \le 3\), when \(c\) and \(d\) are already adjacent (a duplicate edge), and when a new triangle would turn past the fold threshold against the quad's normal (a non-convex quad).

Features and smoothing

MFTLDetectFeatures recomputes tris.normal and classifies, in linear passes over undirected edges (a fan walk over outgoing half-edges misses the closing edge of an open fan):

  • an edge is a feature if \(\hat{\mathbf n}_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}_2 < \cos\theta_\text{feature}\), or if it is a mesh-boundary edge (a missing twin on a ghost triangle is the halo rim, not a boundary; MFTL_FLAG_BOUNDARY decides there);
  • a vertex with exactly two feature edges \(\mathbf d_1, \mathbf d_2\) is a ridge point iff \(-\mathbf d_1\cdot\mathbf d_2 / (\lVert\mathbf d_1\rVert\lVert\mathbf d_2\rVert) > \cos\theta_\text{corner}\); with one, three or more, or a sharper turn it is a corner.

feature_corner_angle is separate from feature_angle_threshold because a boundary is a feature curve at any threshold: without it the four corners of an open patch are ridge points that slide off the patch.

Smoothing is a projection

Class Subspace Displacement
free tangent plane \((\mathbf I - \hat{\mathbf n}\hat{\mathbf n}^\top)\,\mathbf d\)
ridge curve tangent \(\boldsymbol\tau\) \((\boldsymbol\Delta\cdot\boldsymbol\tau / \lVert\boldsymbol\tau\rVert^2)\,\boldsymbol\tau\)
corner none \(\mathbf 0\)

For a free vertex \(\mathbf d = \sum_i A_i\mathbf c_i/\sum_i A_i - \mathbf p\) over the incident triangles (MFTL_SMOOTH_TRIANGLE_BASED; area and centroid offset both come from the two edge vectors leaving \(\mathbf p\)), or the umbrella mean of the neighbours (MFTL_SMOOTH_VERTEX_BASED); \(\hat{\mathbf n}\) is the area-weighted vertex normal, refreshed every sweep, and a vertex whose normal is zero stays put. For a ridge vertex with offsets \(\mathbf d_1, \mathbf d_2\) to its feature neighbours, \(\boldsymbol\tau = \mathbf d_2 - \mathbf d_1\) and \(\boldsymbol\Delta = \tfrac12(\mathbf d_1+\mathbf d_2)\). Moving it to \(\boldsymbol\Delta\) instead cuts the chord: a regular 24-gon ridge shrinks to \(\cos^{20}(\pi/24) = 0.842\) of its radius in 20 sweeps (test_a_regular_ridge_is_a_fixed_point asserts drift below \(10^{-12}\)).

Figure 4. Smoothing is a projection, and the feature class chooses the subspace. (a) A free vertex moves by the tangential part of \(\mathbf{d}\) (drawn at 1.8×; the step is \(\sigma\mathbf{d}\)). (b) A ridge vertex moves along \(\boldsymbol\tau\) only; the full \(\boldsymbol\Delta\) would cut the chord. (c) A corner does not move. (d) Moving a regular 24-gon ridge to its midpoints shrinks it to \(\cos^{20}(\pi/24) = 0.842\) of its radius in 20 sweeps; the projection leaves it fixed.

The area weight is the mechanism that equalises triangle size; without it the operator is nearly the umbrella one (docs/analysis.md §4: 6.0 % volume loss against 3.5 %). Updates are Jacobi (independent of pool order), and the step is

\[ \mathbf p \leftarrow \mathbf p + \sigma\mathbf d,\qquad \sigma = \min\!\big(\lambda,\ \phi\,\ell^\text{fan}_{\min}/\lVert\mathbf d\rVert\big), \]

with \(\lambda\) = smooth_relaxation, \(\phi\) = smooth_max_step_fraction: the projection makes a fold unlikely, not impossible, where the normal is ill-defined. All differences are minimum-image vectors and positions are wrapped. The sweep reads ghost and frozen vertices but moves neither, which makes a distributed sweep exact without communication.

Feature detection on a smooth surface

On a sphere every detected feature is a false positive, and it removes degrees of freedom a vertex needed: in the LeVeque test four spurious edges dropped the worst triangle from 0.797 to 0.045, where it froze, because a constrained vertex is exempt from smoothing, collapse and flip. Final minimum quality 0.76 with detection off against 0.0002 at 45°, monotone over five thresholds (docs/analysis.md §4). Set feature_angle_threshold to 180 for smooth closed interfaces; boundaries remain features.

Compaction

A collapse cannot free anything, so compaction is the deallocator. Half-edges move as triples with their triangle, which preserves implicit indexing and the implicit half-edge ids, and one allocation per pool means no sub-array can be forgotten. out_he_idx is re-derived from the surviving half-edges rather than remapped, so an index an operator failed to repair cannot survive.

During a parallel sweep appended entities sit past the ghosts,

[ owned ...... | ghosts ...... | created this sweep ...... ]
^ n_local

violating the ownership prefix (invariant 2 of mftl_types.h) until compaction swaps the two trailing blocks. That is why the parallel gate is MFTL_FLAG_FROZEN, not index >= n_local.

Splits create and collapses retire ids, so ids stay unique but stop being dense; since redistribution sizes its arrival tables from next_*_gid, every pass ends with dense ids. Pool indices and global ids are therefore both unstable across a remesh: nothing external may hold either.

Distributed remeshing: the obstacle

An operator has a modified set (split, flip: the two incident triangles; collapse: \(a\), \(b\) and \(a\)'s whole fan) and an approval set it must read (for a collapse \(N(a)\cup N(b)\) and both fans). A rank may act iff it owns the modified set exclusively and sees the approval set completely. An edge whose endpoints two ranks own satisfies neither; a lock/ask protocol would put a round trip in the innermost loop and make the result depend on message timing. Two exact strategies avoid negotiation altogether:

SPANNING (remesh_parallel.c) DISPERSED (remesh_dispersed.c)
regime \(R_b \gtrsim h\) \(R_b \le 0.4\,h\)
what moves the cut planes the bodies
placements ≈ 4 at budget 4; 4–19 for a one-shot remesh 1
migrations one per placement (identity skipped), plus one exactly 2
frozen \(O(\delta/h)\) of the mesh nothing
id cost per-body prefix sums over the holders, per placement one per-body MAX
weakness placements, band precondition idle ranks when \(B < P\)

They share no code past MFTLRemeshIterate (different migrations, id schemes and convergence criteria), so test_the_two_strategies_reach_the_same_mesh checks the answer: within 4 % in triangle count and 1 % in volume (measured 0.4–2.5 % and 0.3 % at \(P\) = 1, 2, 4, 8). The tolerance must not become equality: split and collapse are order-dependent.

Figure 5. The two distributed strategies. (a) A body narrower than a rank box moves whole to the rank owning its centroid and is remeshed serially: nothing shared, nothing frozen. (b) A body wider than a rank box stays; the cut planes move by half a box instead. (c) Clustered bodies are spread one hop along comm_graph by a negotiation that never pushes a rank past its cap.

The box is the narrowest rank box, or the coarsened box (Coarsening as a yardstick). dispersed_radius_fraction is 0.4 rather than 0.5 because at \(R = 0.5h\) a body centred on a box corner reaches two boxes diagonally and the centering migration leaves the 26-neighbourhood; the margin absorbs one pass of growth. The spanning driver

Run the unmodified serial kernel only where the whole modified neighbourhood is this rank's alone, then move the cut planes by half a box and run again: what was on a boundary is now in somebody's interior. Nothing overlaps, so nothing is negotiated and the result does not depend on timing (digests are bit-identical on repeated runs, docs/analysis.md §5.3).

The geometric guarantee

Placement \(s\in\{0,1\}^3\) puts the planes of axis \(k\) at \(x_k = \min_k + s_k h_k/2 + j h_k\). A point within \(\delta\) of a plane of one placement is at least \(h_k/2 - \delta\) from every plane of the other, so it is interior there iff

\[ \frac{h_k}{2} - \delta > \delta \iff \boxed{h_k > 4\delta}. \]

Under this condition a point is near the planes of at most one placement per axis: for each axis there is at most one bad value \(b_k\), and the point is interior in placement \(s\) iff \(s_k \ne b_k\) on every axis where \(b_k\) is defined.

(c) On one axis, a point within \(\delta\) of a plane of one placement is at least \(h/2-\delta\) from every plane of the other, so it is interior there when \(h > 4\delta\).

The phase table

Case axes in conflict measure placements that cover every case
slab 1 \(O(\delta/h)\) \(\{000, 111\}\)
tube 2 \(O((\delta/h)^2)\) an orthogonal array of strength 2: \(\{000, 011, 101, 110\}\)
corner 3 \(O((\delta/h)^3)\) all of \(\{0,1\}^3\) (only \(\bar b\) works)

MFTL_REMESH_PHASES orders \(\{0,1\}^3\) so that prefixes are useful: 000, 111 (all slabs), 011, 101, 110 (with 000: all tubes), 100, 010, 001 (all corners). Two placements handle all but \(O((\delta/h)^2)\) of the mesh; the tail rows find a handful of entities and are cheap, because a silent MFTLRemeshIterate stops after one iteration. On a grid with an uncut axis rows coincide (2 distinct placements on \(2\times1\times1\), 4 on \(2\times2\times1\)).

The frozen share

A frozen entity is migrated and then not touched, so the frozen share is the share of every migration that buys nothing. The uniform model \(f = 1 - \prod_k (1 - 2\delta/h_k)\) gives 87.5 % at the limit \(h = 4\delta\), and over-predicts by about two in practice (71 % against 36.6 % measured at \(P = 27\)) because a body does not sample the planes uniformly. Use the measured MFTL_RemeshStats::frozen_fraction.

"Frozen" is derived locally, and exactly

\[ \text{owned entity frozen} \iff \text{another rank holds a copy of it}, \]

and a rank evaluates this alone from the rule redistribution sends by:

\[ t \text{ replicated} \iff \exists k:\ \texttt{tris.verts}[3t{+}k] \ge n^V_\text{local} \ \lor\ \texttt{tris.neighbors}[3t{+}k] \ge n^T_\text{local}, \qquad v \text{ frozen} \iff \exists\, t \ni v \text{ frozen}. \]

Two 12 B/triangle streams and six integer compares; MFTL_ERROR ( \(-1\)) is below every count, so a genuine boundary does not freeze a triangle. Exactness makes a dilation unnecessary:

  1. an unfrozen vertex has only unfrozen (owned, unreplicated) incident triangles;
  2. an unfrozen triangle has only owned corners, so every vertex an approval reads is owned;
  3. the halo is vertex-complete, so every fan an approval walks is complete and gives the answer it would give on the whole mesh.

Hence the gates are two flag tests: freeze_blocks_pair (split, flip: either incident triangle frozen) and freeze_blocks_collapse (either endpoint frozen). And every ghost is frozen on its owner (it is here because this rank owns a corner or edge neighbour of it), so ghost copies are still exact after the sweeps and no ghost position sync is needed inside the loop (ghosts_agree_with_owners in tests/test_remeshing_parallel.c; frozen_matches_replication checks the equivalence both ways — "replicated but not frozen" is the fatal direction).

The band is measured

The freeze rule's reach bounds \(\delta \le (3 + \texttt{rings})\,\ell_{\max}\), but the band actually observed on a sphere in \([-1,1]^3\) is \(\approx 0.84\,\ell_{\max}\) for targets 0.06–0.50; at target 0.12 the bound predicts \(4\delta = 1.92 > h = 1\) while the pass converges with nothing hidden. So local_band_probe measures it: the minimum distance over axes to the current placement's planes, maximised over frozen vertices and over all phases (a coarsening pass lengthens edges, and the band grows: 0.216 at phase 0, 0.375 later at target 0.40).

A shifted plane needs a floor

MFTLRemeshOwnerProc computes \(\sigma = (x_k - \min_k - \text{shift}_k)/h_k\). Without a shift \(\sigma \ge 0\) and (int) is \(\lfloor\cdot\rfloor\); with a shift \(\sigma\) reaches \(-0.5\), where (int) truncates towards zero and gives the sliver \([\min_k, \min_k + \text{shift}_k)\) to cell 0 instead of cell \(\texttt{cuts}_k - 1\) across the seam. On a periodic axis the index is therefore \(\lfloor\sigma + \texttt{cuts}_k\rfloor \bmod \texttt{cuts}_k\). The wrong rule is still a consistent rule — only the seam plane never moves, so entities on it are frozen in every phase (found by mutation testing; test_a_sphere_on_the_periodic_seam_remeshes_everywhere).

For the same reason MFTLRemeshMeasureBodies averages a periodic coordinate as an angle,

\[ \bar\theta_k = \operatorname{atan2}\Big(\sum_t A_t\sin\theta_{k,t},\ \sum_t A_t\cos\theta_{k,t}\Big), \qquad \theta = \frac{2\pi(c-\min_k)}{\text{size}_k}; \]

the arithmetic mean puts a seam-straddling body in the middle of the domain with half the domain as radius (test_a_seam_straddling_droplet_is_not_measured_as_domain_sized).

Global ids

Ids must be unique across ranks and dense at the end (a rank-encoded 64-bit id would be sparse by construction).

Per placement. The operators draw ids from the object's counter, so two ranks that both split an edge of a body produce the same id — harmless, because nothing communicates before the fix-up. Entities created in the phase are those with \(\texttt{gid} \ge w\), the watermark recorded before the sweeps. With \(c_r\) the count this rank created and \(\mathrm{Exscan}\) the exclusive prefix sum over the body's holders in ascending rank order (MFTLEnsembleExscanBodies),

\[ \texttt{gid} \leftarrow w + \mathrm{Exscan}(c_r) + \text{local ordinal}, \qquad w' = w + \textstyle\sum_r c_r . \]

A scan, not a reduction: two holders need different ranges, which no combination provides. Its ranges tile \([w, w')\) exactly (test_the_scan_tiles_every_body_exactly_once).

At the end. redensify_global_ids renumbers owned entities by slot, \(\texttt{gid}(v) = \mathrm{Exscan}(n^V_\text{local}) + v\) (likewise triangles, half-edges \(3\,\texttt{gid}(t)+k\)), in a forced order: scan first; then MFTLRemeshSyncGhostIds, keyed on the OLD id (renumbering first would make every request unanswerable, and ghosts would silently keep stale ids); overwrite last. Ghost triangles are not exchanged: the closing redistribution re-receives them, which is why it comes after.

The convergence certificate

Counting "approved, then refused by the gate" reported 3 permanently blocked operations on a converged \(P = 4\) sphere: around a ghost vertex the one-ring is incomplete, so a rank approves collapses the owner rejects. The gate therefore runs first, and the certificate counts out-of-band edges hidden by it, each edge attributed to the owner of the incident triangle with the smaller global id (so it is counted once mesh-wide):

  • \(\text{hidden}_\text{long}\): \(\ell > \ell_{\max}\) and an incident triangle frozen (the split gate);
  • \(\text{hidden}_\text{short}\): \(\ell < \ell_{\min}\) and an endpoint frozen (the collapse gate).
Exit Test Why sound
fast ops \(= 0\) ∧ hidden \(= 0\) every out-of-band edge was offered to a rank allowed to act, and it declined on merit
slow ops \(= 0\) ∧ hidden \(_\text{long} = 0\) for n_distinct consecutive placements a covering set: under \(h > 4\delta\) every entity was interior in one of them

\(\text{hidden}_\text{long}\) is exact (an offered long edge is always split), so non-zero resets the streak; \(\text{hidden}_\text{short}\) has false alarms (a short edge no collapse may remove, sitting on a band), which the slow exit absorbs. The certificate is measured before the sweeps of a phase, so the smoothing after the last sweep can still move a few edges out of the band.

Coarsening

When more ranks shrink \(h\) below \(4\delta\), bands along the cut planes are silently never remeshed. A coarser decomposition needs no second communicator, because redistribution accepts any destination per entity (MFTL_OwnerMap): MFTLRemeshCoarseOwnerProc maps a fine cell \(i_k\) to the representative of its group, \(\lfloor i_k/g_k\rfloor g_k\), with the remainder absorbed into the last group (a short group would have a narrower \(H\) and could re-create a frozen seam). The covering argument holds with \(H_k = g_k h_k\), and

\[ g_k = \min\!\big(\texttt{cuts}_k,\ \lceil 4\delta/h_k\rceil\big) \]

per axis: a domain thin in \(x\) loses a factor 2, not 8. parallel_coarsen = 1 (default) arms the decision; on a healthy partition the factors are \(\{1,1,1\}\) and the mesh is bit-identical (test_the_fallback_stays_off_on_a_healthy_partition compares mesh_digest; arming costs about 0.5 % of a timestep). When active, \(1/G\) of the ranks do all the sweeping; a longer target length or the dispersed strategy is cheaper when available.

Trigger Fires when Default Character
BAND \(h_k \le 4\delta\) on a cut axis on required; the alternative is an un-remeshed lattice
FROZEN frozen_fraction > coarsen_frozen_fraction off trade: 36.6 % frozen → converged instead of 32 hidden edges, for 2× wall clock
GATHER owned triangles \(\le\) serial_gather_max_triangles off trade: crossover ~50–100 triangles per rank

GATHER short-circuits the others with \(g_k = \texttt{cuts}_k\); BAND and FROZEN are evaluated through the same MFTLRemeshCoarseningFactors, FROZEN by inflating the band: requiring each of \(n\) cut axes to keep an unfrozen share \(u = (1-f^*)^{1/n}\) gives \(g_k = \lceil 2\delta/((1-u)h_k)\rceil\), i.e. the band \(\delta' = \delta/(2(1-u))\). A cap parallel_coarsen > 1 shrinks the largest factor first and reports once if it binds below what BAND required. The decision uses \(\max(\text{measured band}, \ell_{\max})\), since a coarsening pass lengthens edges.

The gather is the safest coarse map: nothing is replicated or frozen, there is one distinct placement, and a pass costs exactly two migrations; every rank sends to one destination. A coarse destination lies outside the 26-neighbourhood, so redistribution and MFTLRemeshSyncGhostIds take their global paths (both detect it). MFTLUpdateGhostPositions has no global fallback, which is why a coarse partition never outlives the call: both drivers close with MFTLRedistribute. Tables: docs/analysis.md §5.6.

The dispersed driver

For bodies smaller than a rank box — emulsions, sprays, bubbly flows — migrate each body whole onto one rank and remesh it serially: no shared edges, no freezing, two migrations per pass.

├─ 5 id counters: per-body MAX over the holders; busiest rank: 1 Allreduce
└─ 6 MFTLRedistribute back to the geometric partition; counts; geometry; profile

Admission is on the radius, not on the outcome. A one-destination migration makes any body local — a domain-spanning jet would arrive whole on one rank, report itself perfectly local and serialise the largest object onto one rank's memory, with a migration that leaves the stencil. Deciding before anything moves also means the spanning fallback never sees a half-remeshed ensemble. The count uses lowest_holder so that a body held by four ranks is counted once (without it the driver printed "4 of 1 bodies are wider than a rank box").

Ids need almost nothing. After centering exactly one rank holds any entity of a body, and ids live in a per-body space, so the owner's counter is unique. The renumbering is unconditional (density is required by the next redistribution), and the other holders learn the counters by a per-body MAX, to which a rank holding nothing contributes zero (not a stale value, which would be too large for a body that shrank).

Load balance

Bodies go where their centroids are, and centroids cluster. Two situations look alike in a profile and call for opposite responses:

\(B \ge P\) \(B < P\)
cause bodies sit in few boxes fewer bodies than ranks
fixable yes: reassign no: a body is the smallest unit of work
read load_imbalance n_ranks_idle

With weights \(w_b\) (global triangle counts),

\[ \text{cap} = \max\!\Big(\texttt{slack}\cdot\frac{\sum_b w_b}{P},\ \max_b w_b\Big), \qquad \texttt{load\_imbalance} = \frac{P\max_r \text{load}_r}{\sum_b w_b}; \]

a cap below the heaviest body would ask for an impossible assignment.

One rank decides each body

MFTL_OwnerMap is undefined if two ranks name different destinations for one entity. Instead of replicating every body's centroid and weight on every rank ( \(O(B)\) per rank), the owner of body \(o\)'s reduced centroid, \(\mathrm{home}(o)\), decides and publishes the verdict one hop along comm_graph. The precondition

\[ \forall o\ \forall \text{holders } H:\quad \mathrm{home}(o) \in \{H\}\cup N(H) \]

is tested by each holder locally and agreed by one reduction. It implies both messages: home can be told (the graph is symmetric and lowest_holder is a holder) and home can tell every holder. A failure (n_bodies_unreachable) falls back to plain centroid ownership, never to replication.

A negotiation, not a greedy

Every overloaded rank sees the same idle neighbour. So a rank advertises its room (cap − load); an overloaded rank asks a few neighbours, emptiest first, for all their room until its excess plus one body is covered; each neighbour grants its real room to askers neediest first, so \(\sum_s \text{grant}_{s\to r} \le \text{room}_r\) and no rank is pushed past its cap. Bodies are placed heaviest first. Two silent failures were found by measurement: a proportional ask over 26 neighbours returns grants smaller than one body, so nothing moves (108 bodies on 27 ranks stuck at imbalance 2.00); and a tie-break identical on every rank starves one target (1 of 8 ranks idle), so ties break on the neighbour's cell offset relative to the asker. Rounds are bounded at three (two sufficed everywhere measured); running out leaves a reported imbalance, never a wrong mesh.

\(4P\) droplets packed into one rank box (remesh_regime_sweep -only l):

No digest can distinguish a replicated from a local balancer — both give a correct, balanced mesh — so the scaling property is tested on the payload: test_the_balancer_payload_ignores_bodies_a_rank_does_not_hold requires a rank holding no body to stage the same number of doubles when the ensemble doubles (25 / 49 / 85 at \(P\) = 2 / 4 / 8). Weight moves at most one cell per pass. The balancer decides who remeshes, not who holds the mesh afterwards.

Coarsening as a yardstick

\(R_{\max} > 0.4\,h_{\min}\) would send a spray to the spanning driver merely because more ranks shrank \(h\). With parallel_coarsen armed the admission test uses the coarsened box \(g_k h_k\); the destinations do not change (coarse routing would restrict them to \(P/G\) ranks and undo the balancer). Unbounded, this is harmful — on a \(2\times1\times1\) grid the coarse box is the whole domain and a jet would be serialised onto one rank — so dispersed_box_min relaxes only when \(B \ge P\) (otherwise ranks are idle anyway) and \(G < P\) (merging everything is the gather, a spanning decision bounded by a triangle count). Chooser and driver use the same function. test_small_bodies_at_a_fine_grid_stay_dispersed checks both directions on a \(\{P,1,1\}\) grid.

The default stays at 32 because a too-small budget fails loudly in the one-shot case, while a too-large one fails quietly in the loop. At budget 4 the placement count saturates (3.83 → 3.96 from \(P\) = 2 to 8) and the overhead on a fixed problem grows 33 % for 4× the ranks; nothing has been measured above \(P = 8\) or on a cluster.
