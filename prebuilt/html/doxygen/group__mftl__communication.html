Detailed Description

Redistribution, ghost synchronisation, and the spatial halo.

What redistribution is

The domain is cut into a Cartesian grid of cuts[0] x cuts[1] x cuts[2] rank boxes. A vertex belongs to the rank whose box contains it; a triangle belongs to the rank whose box contains its centroid. Redistribution moves every entity to its owner and rebuilds the halo around what stayed.

The halo rule, and why it is stronger than "one ring"

A triangle is sent to the owner of each of its three corners and to the owner of each of its three edge neighbours. The corner clause is what makes the halo vertex-complete: for every owned vertex, every triangle incident to it is present locally. That is precisely the precondition MFTLComputeVertexNormals() needs to be communication-free, and it is a strictly stronger statement than "one layer of edge-adjacent ghost triangles".

The same rule read backwards defines replication: an owned entity is replicated on another rank iff it has a ghost corner or a ghost edge neighbour, i.e. 

corner >= verts.n_local  ||  neighbour >= tris.n_local

Two streaming reads, six integer compares, no distance test and no message. That is what MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen() computes; see Isotropic surface remeshing.

What survives a redistribution

Redistribution invalidates all cached geometry except the vertex positions. It returns an object with valid topology, valid positions and MFTL_DERIVED_TOPOLOGY set; tris.area/normal/center and verts.normal hold unspecified values until something recomputes them, and MFTL_DERIVED_GEOMETRY is clear. That is what buys the 3× smaller wire format. A body whose geometry bit is clear when the next redistribution starts is recomputed before it is routed.

The spatial halo is a different thing entirely

The redistribution halo is TOPOLOGICAL: neighbouring triangles of the same body. The spatial halo (MFTLExtendSpatialHalo()) is GEOMETRIC: the triangles of body B that some other rank holds near this rank's share of body A. It is delivered as a standalone shadow MFTL_Object per body, deliberately NOT in ens->local_objects, so redistribution and both ghost syncs never see it.

Note
An extent is a centre plus a half-width, never a (lo, hi) box. Two shares meeting across a periodic seam are a domain length apart in stored coordinates, so a min/max box says they do not touch; and a share holding one seam-straddling triangle has a box spanning the whole domain. "Near" is the minimum-image distance between centres against the sum of half-widths.
