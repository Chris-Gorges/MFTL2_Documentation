Detailed Description

Navigation over the half-edge structure, and connectivity maintenance.

The half-edge data structure

An oriented manifold triangle mesh is stored as a set of half-edges: each undirected edge is split into two oppositely directed halves, one belonging to each incident triangle. A half-edge \(h\) carries

\[ \mathrm{target}(h), \qquad \mathrm{twin}(h) \]

and the classical structure adds \(\mathrm{next}(h)\) and \(\mathrm{face}(h)\). MFTL stores neither, because of

Implicit indexing

Half-edge \(3t+k\) is corner \(k\) of triangle \(t\). Therefore

\[ \mathrm{face}(h) = \left\lfloor h/3 \right\rfloor, \qquad \mathrm{next}(h) = 3\left\lfloor h/3 \right\rfloor + (h+1) \bmod 3, \qquad \mathrm{prev}(h) = 3\left\lfloor h/3 \right\rfloor + (h+2) \bmod 3 \]

and the origin follows from the target of the previous half-edge, \(\mathrm{origin}(h) = \mathrm{target}(\mathrm{prev}(h))\). Three consequences:

  • a whole family of "the next-cycle is broken" corruptions cannot occur, which is why MFTLCheckMeshIntegrity() is a third of its usual size;
  • the half-edge array needs no capacity of its own — it is \(3\times\) the triangle capacity, in the same allocation;
  • the price is that the mesh is triangles-only, which it already was.

Walking a vertex fan

The one-ring of a vertex is generated by alternating the two involutions: from an outgoing half-edge \(h\), the next outgoing half-edge around the same origin is

\[ h' = \mathrm{next}(\mathrm{twin}(h)) \]

which closes into a cycle on an interior vertex and terminates at MFTL_ERROR on a boundary vertex. A boundary vertex therefore has one more neighbour vertex than it has incident triangles: the closing edge of an open fan is reachable only as an incoming half-edge. MFTLGetVertexOneRingVertices() handles that; MFTLGetVertexOneRingHalfedges() deliberately does not, because its contract is outgoing half-edges.

Boundary is not the same as "no twin"

twin_idx == MFTL_ERROR means "no partner in **this** partition" — true at the rim of an open surface and at the outer edge of a ghost halo. MFTL_FLAG_BOUNDARY means "no partner anywhere in the mesh"; it is written once by MFTLMarkMeshBoundary() while the mesh is still complete, and from then on travels with the entity.
