Ownership and the halo rule

A vertex belongs to the rank whose Cartesian cell contains its position; a triangle to the rank whose cell contains its cached centre (a body whose MFTL_DERIVED_GEOMETRY bit is clear is recomputed first) ( \(i_k = \lfloor (x_k-\min_k)\,\mathrm{cuts}_k/L_k\rfloor\)). An owned triangle is additionally sent to the owner of each of its three corners and each of its three edge neighbours; that surplus is the ghost layer. Two properties follow and every other module relies on them:

  • Vertex completeness. Every triangle incident to an owned vertex is present locally (the corner clause). Vertex normals are therefore exact on owned vertices without communication, and every fan a remesher walks around an unfrozen vertex is complete.
  • Edge completeness. Every owned triangle has all three edge neighbours locally (the neighbour clause), so a missing twin on an owned triangle really is a mesh boundary.

Read backwards, the rule characterises replication locally:

\[ \text{owned triangle replicated} \iff \exists\,\text{corner} \ge \texttt{verts.n\_local}\ \lor\ \exists\,\text{neighbour} \ge \texttt{tris.n\_local}, \]

which is the remesher's freeze test (`src/remeshing/` — Isotropic surface remeshing).

Figure 1. The halo rule on an open patch split between two ranks. (a) A vertex belongs to the rank whose cell holds it, a triangle to the rank holding its centre. (b) Rank 0's pool: its owned triangles plus every triangle it receives because it owns a corner (corner clause) or an edge neighbour (neighbour clause) of it. Every owned vertex has its complete fan (coral), so vertex normals need no communication. (c) Read backwards: an owned triangle is replicated, and so frozen for the remesher, exactly when one of its corners or neighbours is a ghost here.

What the wire carries

Only topology, identity and vertex positions travel:

Record Contents Size
MFTL_PackedVertex position, global id, new owner, flags, body id 40 B
MFTL_PackedTriangleComplex global id, new owner, flags, body id, 3 target-vertex ids, 3 half-edge flags 40 B

Areas, normals and centres are functions of positions and topology and are not sent (shipping them would triple the payload). Half-edge ids ( \(3\,t_\mathrm{gid}+k\)), next/face (arithmetic), twins and out_he_idx are reconstructed at the receiver. vertex_gid[k] is the target of half-edge \(3t+k\), not tris.verts[3t+k] (its origin). Boundary flags travel because a partition cannot re-derive them. The MPI datatypes are one or two contiguous blocks with extents pinned to the C sizeof (MPI_Type_create_resized), guarded by static assertions.

Postcondition. A redistributed object has valid topology, valid positions and MFTL_DERIVED_TOPOLOGY set; tris.area/normal/center and verts.normal are unspecified until recomputed (The per-timestep loop).

The redistribution algorithm

  1. Classification. compute_owners streams verts.pos and tris.center through MFTLGetOwnerProc (or the caller's MFTL_OwnerMap) and writes the new owner into owner_proc, counting changes as it goes.
  2. Destination analysis. Each owned triangle's destinations are its owner, its corners' owners and its neighbours' owners. Interior triangles, whose seven candidates coincide, take a register-only fast path. This rank is one destination among others: entities it keeps are flagged, not packed. Each destination's vertex list is de-duplicated on construction with one bit per (vertex, destination) — 32 slots suffice on the neighbour route; beyond them a list is sorted instead.
  3. Route. One MPI_Allreduce (LOR) decides whether any destination lies outside the 26-neighbourhood. Graph route: MPI_Neighbor_alltoallv over comm_graph; global route (e.g. the initial scatter from rank 0): MPI_Alltoallv over comm_cart.
  4. Exchange. MFTLExchangeStart sends three payloads — vertices, triangles, and a 7-int header per (body, destination) — after one merged count exchange. All bodies share the messages, so the message count is independent of the body count, and the headers let a receiver split the stream into per-body runs without sorting.
  5. Rebuild. Kept entities are compacted in place into [kept owned | new owned | kept ghost | new ghost] (ownership stays a prefix); arrivals are de-duplicated by global id and appended. A twin between two kept triangles is a remap; only half-edges whose twin did not survive are re-paired. Kept-ness is decided per triangle, so this repair set is closed under the twin relation and the incremental repair finds every pair a full MFTLRebuildTwins would (checked by re-running it in tests/test_redistribution.c). out_he_idx, tris.verts and tris.neighbors are emitted inline. Bodies that arrive are created; bodies left empty are destroyed.

All scratch lives in a persistent per-ensemble workspace that only grows, so a steady loop stops allocating after a few steps.

The global-id table

"Do I already hold global id \f$g\f$, and in which slot?" is asked for every arriving entity. Ids are dense per body, but a rank holds a scattered subset of a body's range, so a direct-address array would be sized by the global count (measured 15–62 MB per rank per step at \(P = 2\)–8 on one 327 680-triangle body, 97 % empty at \(P = 8\)). mftl_gid_table therefore has two modes, chosen per use:

  • direct (tag[gid]) when \(\text{range} \le 4\,n_\text{entries} + 64\);
  • sparse: open addressing with linear probing, load factor \(\le 1/2\), a multiplicative hash (the identity index clusters catastrophically on a partition's aliased id runs).

This is the library's only hash table. Entries carry an epoch in the high 32 bits of their tag, so retiring every entry is epoch++; nothing is ever deleted (a probe stops at the first stale slot); capacity is a power of two in both modes, so switching modes between bodies never reallocates.

A body that did not move is not touched

A rank's state after a redistribution is a pure function of the global topology and the owner map — read backwards, the halo rule defines the output. Positions only decide the owner map. So a body for which neither input changed already holds the answer, and MFTLRedistributeSetSkipUnchangedBodies (default on) skips its analysis, packing and rebuild entirely (docs/analysis.md §2.4: 1.6–2.1× of the redistribution phase at 1000 bodies).

The precondition is not "did topology change". Under the remesher's freeze discipline a topology change touches only unreplicated entities and is invisible to the halo rule. What a skip breaks is global ids and flags of replicated entities (renumbering relies on the rebuild to refresh ghost-triangle ids). The precondition is therefore no entity changed owner and not partition_dirty — the stamp set by MFTLObjectSetCounts (every creation and destruction), by the renumbering sites and by MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen.

Agreement. A rank may receive a change it cannot see, so the verdict is agreed per body — with one per-body MAX reduction over each body's holders rather than one collective per body. The object loop is split: pass 1a classifies every body and packs those this rank saw change; the vote; pass 1c packs bodies dirty elsewhere by re-running the idempotent classification.

A body must be clean twice. A rebuild is not idempotent (an owned vertex whose incident triangles are all remote is dropped and re-received at the end of the owned run), so a body is rebuilt once more after it stops changing, and only then skipped:

changed now changed before action layout
yes — rebuild not necessarily a fixed point
no yes rebuild ("settle") fixed point
no no skip unchanged fixed point

The skip is then exact slot for slot (test_skipping_reproduces_the_full_rebuild compares set and slot-order digests), at the cost of one rebuild per dirty-to-clean transition (n_objects_settled).

Memory policy

Pools only grow; MFTLRedistributeSetShrinkSlack (default off) offers every rebuilt body to MFTLObjectShrinkToFit. Bodies a rank no longer holds at all are always released. After generation (every body built whole on rank 0) this reduced rank 0's pool capacity from 80 MB to 33.5 MB at 256 bodies, refine 3, \(P = 4\); the optional shrink saves a further ~8 % for ~2 % of the phase time.

Ghost synchronisation

MFTLUpdateGhostPositions / MFTLUpdateGhostVertexNormals run one request/reply round over comm_graph: each rank lists its ghost vertices (the pool suffix) by owner and sends (body id, vertex id) requests (8 B); owners answer through the id table with an MFTL_GhostUpdatePayload (32 B); replies return in request order and are applied by direct index. Three neighbourhood collectives. Only the holder of a ghost knows it holds it, so a request phase is unavoidable; no owner-to-holder state is cached between calls. One field per exchange: bundling recomputable quantities with the position would ship bytes the receiver computes more cheaply.

Warning
A ghost whose owner is not a graph neighbour cannot be served: it keeps a stale value, the call returns MFTL_ERROR on that rank, and Debug builds assert. There is no global fallback, so a partition whose halo leaves the 26-neighbourhood (a coarsened remeshing partition) must not outlive the remeshing call; both remeshing drivers end with a plain MFTLRedistribute.

The spatial halo

Redistribution halo Spatial halo
kind topological geometric
contents neighbouring triangles of the same body triangles of body B near this rank's share of body A
lives in the body's own pools, past n_local a standalone shadow MFTL_Object per body
built by MFTLRedistribute MFTLExtendSpatialHaloNearBodies / MFTLExtendSpatialHalo

Two bodies in contact are cut by the same rank boundaries, and the rank holding one side of a contact may hold nothing of the other body; they share no edges, so no topological halo growth can help. The halo is transient: redistribution keeps only owned triangles, so it is rebuilt after every redistribution, and each extension replaces earlier patches.

Why a shadow object. A patch is an ordinary object with n_local == 0, twins linked within the patch and derived arrays built; it is not in ens->local_objects, so redistribution and ghost syncs never see it. Appending foreign triangles to the body's own ghost suffix would silently change every pass bounded by tris.n (the Morton box, MFTLMeanEdgeLength, the vertex-normal scatter, MFTLRebuildTwins — which would weld bodies sharing vertex slots), and the arriving vertex ids would violate either vertex completeness or local id uniqueness. Patch boundary flags are the transmitted ones: a patch's rim is a cut, not a mesh boundary.

Records, extents and the pair broad phase

  1. Each rank publishes one record per body it holds — (id, centre, half-width, global radius, envelope), nine doubles — to its graph neighbours (payload \(O(\text{local bodies}\times\text{degree})\), independent of the ensemble size).
  2. Extents are a centre plus a half-width, and "near" is

    \[ \forall k:\ \bigl|\Delta^{\min}_k(c^A_k, c^B_k)\bigr| \le h^A_k + h^B_k + m, \]

    with \(\Delta^{\min}\) the minimum-image difference. A (lo, hi) box fails both ways on a periodic domain: shares touching across the seam are a domain length apart (a missed contact, decided before the BVH ever sees the triangles), and a share containing one seam-straddling triangle spans the whole domain (every neighbour ships everything — measured at \(P = 27\): 314 ghosted triangles against 56). A share's extent is measured in the raw frame and in one shifted by half a domain, keeping the tighter per axis, which is exact while the share is narrower than \(L/2\) and conservative otherwise.
  3. The arriving records are binned into a uniform grid with cell \(\ge 2\,h_{\max} + m_{\max}\) per axis, so overlapping extents are in adjacent cells and each query visits 27 cells: \(O(\text{bodies}+\text{records})\) instead of the \(O(B^2)\) all-pairs list (134 million records at 16 384 bodies). One domain-spanning body collapses the grid to a single cell — correct, and the right cost for a few large bodies.
  4. Body B travels towards body A only when \(A < B\) (and \(A = B\) with self_pairs): the rank owning the A-side triangle reports the contact, so only it needs the copy. Per destination each triangle is packed once however many partners want it, and the margin used is the widest any matched partner needs — the pre-filter is always at least as permissive as the per-triangle test.
  5. halo->pair returns every (A, B) with this rank holding A and seeing B, ascending and unique. Measured on six touching couples (6 of 66 pairs real): 6/6/9/18/6 candidates at \(P = 1, 2, 4, 8, 27\) against 66/132/264/528/1782 for all pairs (test_the_broad_phase_finds_the_pairs_without_enumerating_them).
Figure 2. Why an extent is a centre and a half-width. One periodic axis, unrolled. (a) Two shares touching across the seam have disjoint \((\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi})\) boxes, so the contact is missed before any triangle is compared. (b) A share straddling the seam has a box spanning the whole domain, so every neighbour ships it everything. Measured in the raw frame and in one shifted by half a period, keeping the tighter, the centre and half-width are right in both cases under the minimum image.

MFTLSpatialHaloResolveRadii computes each body's radius — half the longest side of its global box (so envelopes do not depend on the process count; \(R\) for a sphere) — over the holders in two frames, and publishes it one hop further, because a rank deciding what to send needs the size of the body it sends towards. A rank two hops from a body neither receives it nor can report a contact with it — the same reach as the exchange.

The directory (MFTL_SpatialHalo) is a sorted body-id array holding the bodies this rank holds plus those it received a patch for, pruned after every exchange (12 slots over all ranks for twelve bodies at \(P = 27\), against 324 for a dense table).

Known limitations

  1. MFTLUpdateGhostPositions builds an [nproc] rank-to-neighbour table per call, and the halo exchange allocates two [nproc] send-buffer descriptor arrays per call — \(O(P)\) per rank and timestep, harmless at the tested scales but a candidate for the persistent workspace at \(P\) in the thousands.
  2. The ghost exchange rebuilds its schedule every call; it could be cached for a solver that redistributes only every \(N\) steps.
  3. The geometry pass after a redistribution runs on freshly rewritten pools and is bound by first touch (6.4 ns/triangle against 2.9 on a resident mesh); fusing it into the rebuild was not attempted.
  4. Nothing has been measured above 4 ranks on real hardware; the scaling of the graph against the global route at \(P\) in the hundreds is unmeasured.
