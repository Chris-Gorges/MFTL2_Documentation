No Matches
Invariants worth preserving

Every entry below is a property that must survive any change, the reason it exists, and the test that guards it. They share one characteristic: breaking any of them is silent. The mesh stays structurally valid, the integrity checks still pass, and the picture still renders — the numbers are just wrong.

Geometry

A normal is always a unit vector, at every scale

MFTLGetTriangleProperties(), src/geometry/triangle_geometry.c, src/ensemble/ensemble_update.c and MFTLNormalizeVector() all compute

\[ m = \sqrt{\norm{\vect{v}}^2} \quad \text{(unclamped)}, \qquad \mathrm{inv} = \begin{cases} 1/m & m > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Two tempting spellings are wrong:

  • **Clamping \(\norm{\vect{v}}^2\) before the square root** floors the magnitude at \(\sqrt{10^{-12}} = 10^{-6}\) — and, where an area is derived from the same \(m\), floors that at \(5\times10^{-7}\). So quality.min_triangle_area could never report a degenerate triangle, and \(q = 4\sqrt{3}A/(a^2+b^2+c^2)\) became unbounded above; an average \(q\) of 1.023 was measured on a degenerate mesh.
  • Clamping the divisor with MFTL_MAX(mag, tiny) keeps the instruction count but only moves the floor. At tiny = MFTL_VERYVERYSMALL a triangle with \(10^{-8}\) edges still came out with a normal of length 0.112.
Warning
A short normal is the most dangerous shape of wrong available here: it points the right way, so every sign test, winding check and picture still passes, while it silently scales surface tension and every other quantity built from it.

The test against zero has no cliff and costs one instruction (fcmp+fcsel against fmax): 7.33 → 7.41 ns/tri, +1.1 %. Pay it.

Guarded by test_triangle_normals_are_unit_at_every_scale ( \(10^{-8}\) … \(10^{8}\), all three kernels), test_normalize_is_unit_across_magnitudes ( \(10^{-150}\) … \(10^{150}\)), test_normals_of_a_whole_mesh_are_unit, test_a_very_small_triangle_is_measured_exactly, and inv_triangle_normals_are_unit / inv_vertex_normals_are_unit in tests/test_mesh_invariants.h — which are deliberately not part of inv_mesh_is_sound(), because redistribution leaves tris.normal unspecified by design.

The expression must stay identical in the walk and stream kernels, or test_triangle_geometry_paths_are_bit_identical fails.

Face normals point outward, everywhere

The icosahedron face table in src/mesh_creation/sphere_creation.c is wound counter-clockwise as seen from outside, and the 1-to-4 subdivision emits every sub-triangle in its parent's rotational order. This is what makes the enclosed volume positive under the divergence theorem and fixes the sign of surface tension and of the indicator function.

Guarded by test_triangle_winding_is_outward, test_enclosed_volume_is_positive_and_converges.

Never compare two identical vectors with acos(a·b)

The arc cosine has infinite derivative at 1, so a dot product one ulp below 1 reports \(1.5\times10^{-8}\) rad — a noise floor of \(\sim10^{-6}\) degrees regardless of how exact the inputs are. Use the chord \(\norm{\vect{a} - \vect{b}}\).

tests/test_vertex_normals.c records the false failure this produced.

Topology

"No twin" and "mesh boundary" are different statements

Only one of them is partition-independent.

Statement Meaning Partition-dependent?
twin_idx == MFTL_ERROR no partner in this partition yes — true at an open surface's rim and at a ghost halo's outer edge
flags & MFTL_FLAG_BOUNDARY no partner anywhere in the mesh no — a property of the surface

MFTL_FLAG_BOUNDARY is written once by MFTLMarkMeshBoundary() while the mesh is still complete, and from then on travels with the entity. Re-deriving it from twin_idx after redistribution would promote every halo rim into a fake boundary.

A boundary vertex has one more neighbour than incident triangles

The closing edge of an open fan is reachable only as an incoming half-edge. MFTLGetVertexOneRingVertices() handles it; MFTLGetVertexOneRingHalfedges() deliberately does not, because its contract is outgoing half-edges.

Guarded by test_one_ring_at_a_boundary_vertex_has_one_extra_neighbour.

A half-edge's global ID is 3 * triangle_gid + k, mesh-wide

Established by MFTLBuildTopologyFromIndexList(), preserved by MFTLObjectReorderMorton() (which moves half-edge triples as a unit) and by redistribution. It is the wire-format half of implicit indexing: half-edge global IDs are never transmitted, the receiver reconstructs them.

Guarded by inv_halfedge_gids_are_implicit.

Global IDs are a dense permutation of [0, n) per entity class

And next_*_gid == n_* after generation. Subdivision retires the whole previous generation of triangles and half-edges, so those counters restart at zero; next_vertex_gid does not, because old vertices are carried over and keep their IDs.

Density is what lets a receiver size a lookup table by ID range instead of hashing — which is why remeshing must renumber at the end of every pass.

Guarded by inv_global_ids_are_dense.

I/O

Only OWNED entities go into a .vtu

A ghost is a copy of a triangle another rank owns, so writing the whole pool puts every halo triangle in the merged dataset once per rank holding it. The picture is unchanged (the copies are coincident) and every integral the visualisation tool computes over the surface is wrong by the halo fraction, silently.

include_ghosts asks for the whole pool deliberately, for looking at the halo.

Guarded by test_ghost_inclusion, test_cell_count_is_the_owned_count.

The periodic-wrap test has no tolerance, on purpose

It asks whether a reconstructed corner \(\vect{p}_0 + \vect{e}\) left the domain box. A false positive costs three extra points carrying correct coordinates and renders identically; a false negative draws a triangle across the whole domain. Any slack added to suppress the first buys the second, so there is none.

Guarded by test_wrap_detection_paths_agree.

MFTLWriteVtu() must never write tris.area or tris.normal

Nothing on the object can say they are current — MFTL_DERIVED_GEOMETRY cannot see vertex motion — and a redistribution leaves them undefined by design, so a production file containing them would look right and be stale.

MFTLWriteVtk() writes tris.area and verts.normal deliberately, because the raw dump's contract is to show what is actually in memory.

Communication

An extent is a centre plus a half-width, never a (lo, hi) box

In src/communication/spatial_halo.c, for what a rank publishes about its share and for each triangle's own contribution. "Near" is the minimum-image distance between centres against the sum of half-widths.

A box gets it wrong in both directions at once:

  • It misses. Two shares meeting across the seam are a domain length apart in stored coordinates, so min/max says they do not touch — and because the classification decides what is sent, the miss lands one stage before anything the periodic image loop can see, so nobody reports the contact.
  • It over-sends. A share holding one seam-straddling triangle has a box spanning the whole domain, because MFTLGetTriangleBounds() is correctly seam-consistent and puts that triangle's corners just outside the box on one side.

Measured at np = 27 on a seam contact: 314 triangles ghosted with boxes against 56 with intervals, and 320 against 0 for a placement with no cross-rank contact at all. The payload is nine doubles per body a rank holds.

A rank's own extent is measured in two frames, raw and shifted by half a domain, keeping the tighter per axis.

Guarded by test_contact_through_the_seam_survives_the_decomposition, test_a_seam_straddling_body_is_not_measured_as_domain_sized.

A pre-filter must be strictly MORE permissive than the test it short-cuts

The halo's per-triangle intervals are widened by the contact margin, so an un-widened macro extent is stricter than what it stands in for and rejects the whole neighbour in exactly the regime the margin exists for.

Overlapping bodies hide it completely — their raw extents intersect anyway — so only a proximity contact at a rank boundary reveals it.

Guarded by test_the_tolerance_threshold_is_exact_from_both_sides.

A spatial ghost is a triangle in a SHADOW OBJECT

MFTLExtendSpatialHalo() puts what the neighbours sent into the halo's patch for that body (MFTLSpatialHaloPatch()) — an ordinary, self-consistent MFTL_Object with n_local == 0, twins linked within the patch, derived arrays built. It is deliberately not in ens->local_objects, so redistribution and both ghost syncs never see it.

Appending them to the body's own ghost suffix instead — which looks free because the ownership prefix is untouched — fails for two reasons that are invisible until they bite:

  1. Four passes bounded by tris.n rather than n_local change silently: the Morton bounding box in reorder_vertices (a partner body across the domain coarsens the cells for the owned mesh), MFTLMeanEdgeLength() (each appended triangle contributes three twin-less half-edges, so the remeshing target length is set partly by another body), the vertex-normal scatter, and MFTLRebuildTwins() (which re-pairs by vertex slot and can weld two bodies together).
  2. There is no good answer for the arriving vertex GIDs. De-duplicate them onto existing slots and inv_halo_is_vertex_complete breaks; give them fresh slots and inv_local_global_ids_are_unique breaks.

Doing it properly needs the two-way ownership split to become three-way, i.e. a change to 2. Ownership is a prefix.

Guarded by test_halo_does_not_touch_the_bodies, test_halo_patch_is_a_valid_object.

Remeshing

MFTL_FLAG_FROZEN means REPLICATED, not "near a cut plane"

An owned entity is frozen exactly when some other rank holds a copy of it, and MFTLRemeshMarkFrozen() derives that from the rule redistribution sent by:

replicated ⟺ corner >= verts.n_local || neighbour >= tris.n_local

Two streaming reads, six integer compares, no distance test and no message. Three things follow, and all three are load-bearing:

  • A vertex is frozen if any incident triangle is, so an unfrozen vertex has every incident triangle owned and unreplicated — which is why gating a collapse, whose modified set is an entire fan, is two flag tests.
  • Combined with the vertex-complete halo, every fan an approval predicate walks is complete, so the link condition, the wing valences and the fold-over test give the same answers they would on the whole mesh.
  • Every ghost in a rank's pool is frozen on its owner. It is here because this rank owns a corner or an edge neighbour of it, which from the owner's side is the definition of replicated. So ghost copies are still exact when the sweeps finish, and a phase needs no ghost position sync before the next migration.

Guarded by ghosts_agree_with_owners (tests/test_remeshing_parallel.c) and frozen_matches_replication — the "replicated but not frozen" direction is the fatal one, and its symptoms appear one or two migrations after the cause.

Note
The precondition this rests on is \(h_k > 4\delta\) per cut axis, and what it buys is that each axis then has at most one bad shift value — which is what makes eight placements a covering set rather than a heuristic. The derivation, the orthogonal-array argument behind the phase table, and what happens when the condition fails are in The geometric guarantee.

A shifted cut plane needs a FLOOR, not a truncation

MFTLRemeshOwnerProc() biases by cuts before the (int) cast on a periodic axis. Without the shift the argument is non-negative for any wrapped position, so (int) is floor; with a shift of up to \(h/2\) it reaches \(-0.5\), where (int) truncates towards zero and attributes the sliver \([\text{min}, \text{min} + \text{shift})\) to cell 0 instead of to cell \(\text{cuts}-1\) across the seam.

Warning
What makes that hard to catch is that the wrong rule is still a rule. Every rank computes the same answer, the partition stays consistent, and every structural invariant still holds. The one thing that changes is that the seam plane never moves, so an entity on it is frozen in every phase of the schedule and ends up in a permanently un-remeshed band.

A body away from the seam never enters the sliver, so both original spanning tests passed under the broken rule. Found by mutation testing.

Guarded by test_a_sphere_on_the_periodic_seam_remeshes_everywhere, which fails on convergence and reports the sphere as an open surface with 78 boundary half-edges.

The same reasoning applies to MFTLRemeshMeasureBodies() one level up: it averages a periodic coordinate as an angle (circular mean, atan2 of the area-weighted mean direction), because the arithmetic mean of a seam-straddling body's coordinates lands in the middle of the domain and its radius comes out as half the box. Nothing fails — the body is simply classified SPANNING forever. Guarded by test_a_seam_straddling_droplet_is_not_measured_as_domain_sized.

Remeshing reaches the network through its own file

Every MPI call the remesher makes is in src/remeshing/remesh_comm.c. test_remeshing_mpi_is_confined — a CMake source check, not a runtime one — fails the build if that stops being true, because no runtime test can see it.

The rule exists because the first version broke it in two places that were individually small, correct and fully tested, and collectively the wrong shape: a feature used a few times per hundred timesteps paid for once per timestep, in the two loops the library is most careful about.

MFTLGetOwnerProc() gained a per-axis cut-plane offset and a periodicity branch. That function is the body of compute_owners, i.e. it runs once per vertex and once per triangle on every redistributed timestep. Measured at 1.70× — 1.79 → 3.05 ns/entity, 42 → 89 instructions for the same loop.

What replaces both is MFTL_OwnerMap — one seam, a NULL pointer on the production path, and one predicted branch per body hoisted out of both loops.

A rank must not judge work it only holds as a GHOST

The first parallel convergence certificate counted "approved, then refused by the freeze gate". It reported 3 permanently blocked operations on an np = 4 sphere that was in fact fully converged: a rank examines every half-edge in its pool, ghosts included, and around a ghost vertex its one-ring is incomplete, so it approves a collapse the owner correctly rejects.

The gate therefore runs before the approval (it is cheaper anyway), and the certificate is count_hidden_edges — out-of-band edges hidden by the freeze gate of the operator that would fix them, which needs no fan walk and so cannot be given a wrong answer by a partial view (The convergence certificate).

Every clause of an approval predicate is silent when removed

MFTLCollapseApproved() refuses on five grounds and none of them announce themselves:

Clause What its absence produces
link condition welds two sheets into a non-manifold vertex whose one-ring walk then silently visits half the fan
fold-over an inverted triangle
stretch bound split and collapse oscillate forever
wing-valence floor a tetrahedron passes the neighbour count and still dissolves
feature/boundary link condition two vertices on a feature curve joined by an edge not on that curve are a chord across the surface; collapsing it pinches the curve inwards

The last two are easy to omit because the obvious form of the link condition does not imply them. On an open patch the feature clause's absence serrates the rim; on a cube it merges two sharp edges into the middle of the face between them.

Guarded by test_collapse_refuses_to_dissolve_a_tetrahedron, test_remesh_of_an_open_patch_keeps_its_rim.

Feature classification must describe the mesh being SMOOTHED

Not the one the sweeps started from. MFTLRemeshObject() calls MFTLDetectFeatures() twice per iteration for this reason.

A split of a rim edge creates a midpoint that is on the rim, and a vertex the smoother believes to be free gets the 2D tangent-plane treatment and slides straight off it — after which the surface has a serrated notch that no structural check can see, because the result is a perfectly valid manifold with a perfectly valid boundary.

Smoothing is a PROJECTION

The feature class chooses the subspace: 2D tangent plane, 1D ridge tangent, 0D locked. Stated that way the three cases are one idea.

Moving a ridge vertex to the midpoint of its two feature neighbours — the full displacement, no projection — is the 2D shrinkage problem reintroduced on a 1D curve: a regular 24-gon ridge collapses to 0.84 of its radius in 20 sweeps, which is a large systematic error applied precisely where the geometry was supposed to be preserved.

Guarded by test_a_regular_ridge_is_a_fixed_point, which asserts drift below \(10^{-12}\).

Two feature edges make a ridge point only if the curve runs STRAIGHT

Counting alone makes the four corners of an open patch ridge points, free to slide along the diagonal between their neighbours — off the patch. feature_corner_angle is a separate threshold from feature_angle_threshold because it applies where that one does not: a mesh boundary is a feature curve at any dihedral threshold, including a disabled one.

Contact detection

Periodicity is two problems, and they need two different fixes

  • Within an object, a triangle straddling a seam has wrapped vertices, so a centroid built from raw coordinates lands in the middle of the domain and its AABB spans the box. Fixed by gathering the current corners and applying minimum-image edges to build every primitive.
  • Between objects, two bodies on opposite faces are neighbours and no per-triangle arithmetic can see it. Fixed by traversing the pair once per periodic image (up to 27, each rejected by one root-AABB test).

A straddling object's root box legitimately spans the domain and is not a bug — same accepted cost, for the same reason, as MFTLObjectReorderMorton().

Guarded by test_periodic_morton_of_a_straddling_object, test_contact_across_a_periodic_face.

The contact envelope scales with the body, not the process count

\[ \tau = \max\big(\texttt{proximity\_floor},\ \texttt{proximity\_factor} \cdot R\big) \]

An absolute envelope (say \(10^{-4}\) for the box inflation and \(10^{-6}\) for the proximity) is correct at exactly one length scale.

\(R\) is half the longest side of the root box, not half its diagonal, so it equals the radius for a sphere and factor means what it says. It is measured over one rank's partition, so the distributed pipeline overwrites it with the body's global radius from a per-body reduction over its holders — otherwise splitting a droplet over eight ranks would change the separation at which contact is detected.

Guarded by test_detection_at_two_disparate_length_scales, test_tolerance_is_independent_of_the_partition.

A distributed contact is reported by the A-side owner, and only that rank

So a BVH is built over owned triangles only (including the redistribution halo makes two ranks report the same contact, exactly as writing ghosts into a .vtu double-counts the surface), only object B's triangles travel, and the traversal skips any leaf pair whose A side is remote.

Get it wrong in one direction and cross-rank contacts are missed; wrong in the other and every cross-rank contact force is applied twice. test_distributed_contacts_match_the_serial_answer compares the gathered set against the answer computed on one rank while it still held the whole mesh, which is the only check that rules out both at once.

MFTL_Orient3D is adaptive; nothing else in src/bvh/ is

It is the only predicate whose sign decides the answer — Guigue–Devillers branches on it up to eight times per pair (six classify the corners against the two planes, two more in check_min_max; the permutation cascade evaluates none of its own, branching on signs already computed), and a wrong sign gives a clean "no intersection" for two triangles that cross. Everything else there degrades continuously.

examples/orient3d_predicate_profile.c measures the trade both ways and the answer is not one-sided: the plain determinant did not misclassify once in 20 000 samples at gaps down to \(10^{-12}\) of the body size (three orders below where a lubrication model works), and only fails at \(10^{-14}\ldots10^{-16}\). It is kept because the cost is ≤ 6.2 % of a full MFTL_TriTriProximity() call (2.00× on the predicate, but the narrow phase is dominated by its fifteen distance sub-tests) and the failure it removes is silent.

Topology changes

The reconstructed surface is independent of the process count

Four things keep it so, each silent when broken because every rank still produces a valid mesh:

  1. each rank rasterises only the triangles it OWNS, and the merge rule (smaller \(|\phi|\), ties towards negative) is commutative and associative, so the merged field equals a serial rasterisation bit for bit;
  2. the event list is canonical (points snapped to a quarter of \(\max(\min\delta, \Delta x)\) and merged on bodies, kind and cell);
  3. component labels are the smallest lattice triangle key, which every holder computes alike;
  4. a marching-cubes vertex is positioned from WRAPPED node indices, so two ranks reaching one lattice edge across a periodic seam produce one position and one owner (test_seam_vertices_get_one_owner_on_every_rank).

tests/test_topology_change.c compares every scene against a rank-0 MPI_COMM_SELF rerun; the surfaces agree to \(10^{-12}\) at np = 1, 2, 4, 8 and 27.

An event is classified by the side its gap is on

Two opposing triangles each BEHIND the other enclose liquid (breakup); each in FRONT, a film of the other phase (coalescence). "Within one body = breakup, between two = coalescence" is wrong both ways: a rolled-up cap coalesces with its own stem, and the liquid film between a drop and a bubble inside it breaks between two bodies. Hence clusters are rasterised as the distance to the UNION of their surfaces, signed by the nearest one.

The size floor is taken twice

On the marching-cubes surface and again after the optional remesh: a satellite that clears the floor as slivers can come out of the remesh as a tetrahedron that the collapse approval refuses to dissolve (test_a_satellite_the_remesher_collapses_is_discarded).

How the tests express these

tests/test_mesh_invariants.h holds the structural checks a mesh must satisfy — implicit indexing, twin symmetry, out_he really outgoing, derived arrays agreeing with the records, ownership prefix, dense global IDs.

Use inv_mesh_is_sound() after any operation rather than re-deriving them, and inv_partition_is_sound() after anything distributed. The latter adds the three statements that only mean something once a mesh is spread over ranks:

  1. local global-ID uniqueness,
  2. a vertex-complete halo (inv_halo_is_vertex_complete), which is exactly what MFTLComputeVertexNormals() needs to be communication-free,
  3. every owned triangle having all three edge neighbours.
