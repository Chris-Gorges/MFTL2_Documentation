The minimum-image convention

On a periodic axis the domain is a torus of length \(L_k\), and the difference of two stored coordinates is not the vector between the points. The shortest representative is

\[ \Delta_k = (p_2 - p_1)_k - L_k\,\mathrm{nearbyint}\!\left(\frac{(p_2-p_1)_k}{L_k}\right), \qquad |\Delta_k| \le L_k/2 , \]

evaluated with the precomputed reciprocal inv_domain_size — one multiply, one round, one fused multiply-subtract, valid for any displacement (the comparison form is valid only for \(|d| < 3L/2\)). Every edge vector in the library is formed this way, so a triangle straddling a periodic face has correct edges although its corners are stored on opposite sides of the box. This requires every edge to be shorter than \(L_k/2\), which any resolved surface satisfies.

Figure 1. A triangle straddling a periodic seam. (a) Its corners are stored wrapped, so raw differences span the domain and the centroid of the stored corners lies in its middle. (b) Every edge is a minimum-image vector, so the triangle has its true shape across the seam; the centroid is built from those offsets and then wrapped, which makes it both the true centroid and a position MFTLGetOwnerProc accepts.

MFTLApplyPeriodicity wraps a position into \([\min_k, \max_k)\) by at most one period — correct for positions wrapped every step. Its perfectly predicted compare-and-shift form beat a branchless variant by 1.34–1.37×.

MFTLGetOwnerProc maps a wrapped position to the rank owning its Cartesian cell, \(i_k = \lfloor (x_k-\min_k)\,\mathrm{cuts}_k/L_k \rfloor\) clamped to the grid, through the precomputed grid_factors and rank_map.

Triangle area, normal and centroid

With minimum-image edges \(\mathbf{e}_1 = \mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_0\), \(\mathbf{e}_2 = \mathbf{p}_2 - \mathbf{p}_0\):

\[ \mathbf{n} = \mathbf{e}_1 \times \mathbf{e}_2, \qquad A = \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert \mathbf{n} \rVert, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{\mathbf{n}}{\lVert \mathbf{n} \rVert}, \qquad \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{p}_0 + \tfrac13(\mathbf{e}_1 + \mathbf{e}_2). \]

One square root serves area and normal. The corners are ordered counter-clockwise seen from outside, so \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\) points outward (Invariants worth preserving). The centroid is built from seam-corrected offsets and then wrapped: it is the true centroid of a straddling triangle and a position MFTLGetOwnerProc accepts.

MFTLComputeTriangleGeometry streams tris.verts when the derived connectivity is current and walks the half-edge records otherwise; the two paths are bit-identical (test_triangle_geometry_paths_are_bit_identical).

A normal is always a unit vector

Every kernel evaluates

const double mag = MFTL_SQRT(nx*nx + ny*ny + nz*nz); /* unclamped */
const double inv = (mag > 0.0) ? 1.0 / mag : 0.0;

Clamping \(\lVert\mathbf{n}\rVert^2\) before the square root floors the magnitude (at \(10^{-6}\) for a \(10^{-12}\) clamp) and with it the area, so a degenerate triangle could not be reported and the quality \(q = 4\sqrt3A/(a^2+b^2+c^2)\) could exceed its maximum of 1; clamping the divisor only moves the floor. Either way small triangles get short normals that still point the right way — every sign test passes while surface forces are silently scaled. The test against zero costs one instruction (+1.1 %) and is exact at every scale ( \(10^{-8}\ldots10^{8}\) in test_triangle_normals_are_unit_at_every_scale).

Vertex normals

\[ \mathbf{N}_v^{\text{area}} \propto \sum_{f \ni v} A_f\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_f = \tfrac12 \sum_{f\ni v} (\mathbf{e}_1\times\mathbf{e}_2)_f, \qquad \mathbf{N}_v^{\text{angle}} \propto \sum_{f \ni v} \alpha_f(v)\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_f . \]

Area weighting is free (the raw cross product carries it) and parallel to the gradient of the enclosed volume on a closed fan,

\[ \frac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}_v} = \tfrac13\sum_{f\ni v} A_f\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}_f , \]

the direction a volume-conserving front-tracking scheme moves vertices along. Angle weighting (Thürmer–Wüthrich) is invariant under re-triangulation of the fan and is the pseudo-normal of the signed-distance theorem below. The corner angles come from one cross product and three dot products via atan2, which is well conditioned where acos is not.

Figure 2. Normals. (a) One square root serves both \(A = \tfrac12\lVert\mathbf{e}_1\times\mathbf{e}_2\rVert\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\); the corners are counter-clockwise from outside, so \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\) points out. (b, c) One uneven fan weighted by area (shade \(\propto A_f\)) and by corner angle (wedges \(\alpha_f\)): here the two vertex normals differ by 27°. The area weighting is the volume gradient; the angle weighting is invariant under re-triangulation of the fan and is the pseudo-normal the signed distance needs.

The pass is a scatter over triangles (flat loop, compact connectivity) rather than a gather over fans (dependent loads, measured 3.4× slower); a vertex→triangle CSR gather was a dead heat and is not stored (docs/analysis.md §1.5). verts.normal stays xyz-interleaved: the three-double read-modify-write is four memory operations per vertex instead of six (1.20–1.25×, examples/layout_benchmark.c).

Exactness needs a vertex-complete halo

The scatter runs over all triangles, ghosts included, so an owned vertex is exact iff every incident triangle is present locally — which the redistribution halo guarantees (a triangle is sent to the owner of each of its corners). This makes the pass communication-free.

Warning
Ghost vertices hold a normalised partial sum, which looks exactly like a real normal. MFTLEnsembleUpdateVertexNormals adds the one ghost exchange that makes them exact.

Closest point and signed distance

MFTLClosestPointOnTriangle implements Ericson's Voronoi-region test: six dot products select one of seven regions (face, three edges, three corners) and only that region's formula is evaluated. It also returns the feature; edge \(k\) is half-edge \(3t+k\). A triangle with \(\sin^2\theta \le 10^{-15}\) at corner 0 is treated as its three edges.

MFTLRasterizeSignedDistance scatters, for each triangle, the value \(\pm\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\rVert\) to every lattice node \(\mathbf{x}\) within the cutoff, where \(\mathbf{q}\) is the closest point and the sign is that of \((\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q})\cdot\mathbf{n}_\text{pseudo}\):

Closest feature Pseudo-normal
face \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T\)
edge \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T + \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{T'}\) ( \(T'\) across the edge; \(T\) alone at a mesh rim)
vertex angle-weighted \(\mathbf{N}_v\) (verts.normal, MFTL_NORMAL_WEIGHT_ANGLE)

For a closed, consistently oriented, non-self-intersecting surface this sign is provably correct (Bærentzen & Aanæs, IEEE TVCG 11(3), 2005); area-weighted vertex normals can fail on thin, sharply folded sheets — precisely where topology changes are detected. Contributions are combined by MFTLSdfMerge (smaller magnitude wins, ties to the negative value): a total order, so the merged field is independent of triangle and rank order and a distributed rasterisation is bit-identical to a serial one.

Figure 3. The closest point on a triangle. (a) Perpendiculars through the corners cut the plane into seven regions: the face, three edges (edge \(k\) is half-edge \(3t+k\)) and three corners. Six dot products select the region before any formula is evaluated. (b) A cross-section through a sharp edge. For \(\mathbf{x}\) in the edge's region the face normal alone gives the wrong sign; the edge pseudo-normal \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_T+\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{T'}\) gives the right one.

Nodes farther than the cutoff from every triangle stay MFTL_SDF_UNKNOWN. Every corner of a lattice cell crossed by the surface lies within one cell diagonal of it, so a cutoff above \(\sqrt3\,\Delta x\) supplies every value marching cubes needs without a flood fill. The lattice is global and periodic (nodes \(g\) and \(g + N\) coincide), and a caller stores a window of it; triangles reach nodes by unwrapped index in their own periodic image.
