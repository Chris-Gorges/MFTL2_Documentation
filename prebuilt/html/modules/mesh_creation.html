    src/mesh_creation/ — Mesh construction¶

    Public header: include/mftl_meshgen.h · Layer: 3 · Module group: mftl_meshgen

    File Job
    mesh_builder.c indexed triangle list → half-edge mesh; the single conversion point
    sphere_creation.c the icosphere, in a collective and a split local/publish form
    marching_cubes.c a generated 256-case table, lattice-keyed extraction, level set → mesh

    There is no mesh reader; one would go through MFTLBuildIndexedMesh.

    One conversion point¶

    MFTLBuildIndexedMesh (positions + indices) and MFTLBuildTopologyFromIndexList (indices over an already filled vertex pool) are the only places raw connectivity becomes an MFTL mesh, so the conventions every module relies on are established once:

    • half-edge \(3t+k\) runs from tri_verts[3t+k] to tri_verts[3t+(k+1)%3], so slot \(k\) of tris.verts is the origin of half-edge \(3t+k\);
    • global ids are \([0, n)\) per class, half-edge ids \(3\,t_\mathrm{gid}+k\), and the next_*_gid counters end one past the last id;
    • twins are linked (MFTLRebuildTwins), the boundary is flagged while the mesh is complete (MFTLMarkMeshBoundary), derived arrays are built;
    • MFTLBuildIndexedMesh and the generators also compute the triangle geometry (area, normal, centre), so a new mesh can be redistributed at once — the first redistribution routes by centres. MFTLBuildTopologyFromIndexList does not: its caller still owns the positions (subdivision projects them afterwards).

    Index lists are validated in Release before anything is modified: an index out of range or a triangle with a repeated corner (a self-edge that could twin with itself) is rejected. The caller owns the winding: the normal \((\mathbf{p}_1-\mathbf{p}_0)\times(\mathbf{p}_2-\mathbf{p}_0)\) points to the side from which the triangle is counter-clockwise.

    The icosphere¶

    The regular icosahedron — vertices \((0, \pm1, \pm\varphi)\) and cyclic permutations, \(\varphi = (1+\sqrt5)/2\), scaled onto the unit sphere — is refined \(r\) times by 1-to-4 midpoint subdivision and then projected once onto the sphere of radius \(R\):

          v2
     /  \
   m2 -- m1
   / \  / \
 v0 -- m0 -- v1

    For a closed triangulation Euler's formula \(V - E + F = 2\) with \(3F = 2E\) gives

    \[ F_r = 20 \cdot 4^{\,r}, \qquad E_r = 30 \cdot 4^{\,r}, \qquad V_r = 10 \cdot 4^{\,r} + 2 . \]
    \(r\) vertices triangles min \(q\) mean \(q\) max/min area \(1 - V_h/V\)
    0 12 20 1.000 1.000 1.00 0.395
    1 42 80 0.985 0.989 1.19 0.127
    2 162 320 0.977 0.994 1.51 0.034
    3 642 1 280 0.975 0.996 1.73 8.6e-3
    4 2 562 5 120 0.974 0.997 1.86 2.2e-3
    5 10 242 20 480 0.974 0.997 1.93 5.4e-4

    (\(q = 4\sqrt3A/(a^2+b^2+c^2)\) is the mean-ratio quality; \(V_h\) the enclosed volume of the polyhedron.) Because the midpoints are projected only once, the flat subdivision of each icosahedron face is mapped centrally onto the sphere: shapes stay nearly equilateral while areas grow towards the face centres by up to a factor of about 2. The volume error decreases by a factor of 4 per level — second order in the edge length \(h \propto 2^{-r}\). For a target edge length, \(r \approx \log_2(1.05\,R / h)\).

    Outward normals. The face table is counter-clockwise as seen from outside and subdivision emits the sub-triangles in their parent's rotational order, so every normal points outward at every level. This fixes the sign of the enclosed volume,

    \[ V = \frac13 \oint_{\partial\Omega} \mathbf{x}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \frac13 \sum_t A_t\,(\mathbf{c}_t\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_t), \]

    of surface tension and of any indicator function (test_triangle_winding_is_outward, test_enclosed_volume_is_positive_and_converges).

    Midpoints without a hash map. An edge's midpoint is identified by a half-edge index: midpoint_map[h] is written for \(h\) and its twin together, so the second triangle on an edge finds the vertex by one array read. Subdivision emits an index list and reuses MFTLBuildTopologyFromIndexList; old vertices keep their indices, midpoints are appended.

    Generation is rank-0 serial¶

    The whole body is built on rank 0 — every other rank holds an empty object — and MFTLRedistribute spreads it. Id counters must be broadcast and global counts reduced: MFTLGenerateSphere does both per body (two collectives each); MFTLGenerateSphereLocal for every body followed by one MFTLPublishGeneratedBodies costs two collectives in total. Consequences:

    • rank 0's memory peak is the whole ensemble (\(3440 \cdot 4^r + 128\) bytes per body); a distributed generator does not exist;
    • the opening scatter is the one redistribution that may leave the 26-neighbourhood of rank 0 (whenever some cell is two or more cells from rank 0's along an axis, e.g. a non-periodic \(3\times3\times3\) or \(4\times1\times1\) grid), and then takes the global route;
    • quantities measured on an empty rank (e.g. MFTLMeanEdgeLength = 0) must not be used as rank-local parameters; the remesher measures its target length collectively (src/remeshing/ — Isotropic surface remeshing).

    Marching cubes¶

    A table generated from one face rule¶

    Instead of the classic hand-tabulated 256 rows, every case is derived from a rule applied independently to each of the cell's six faces. Walking a face's four corners counter-clockwise as seen from outside the cell, the sign changes at 0, 2 or 4 edges; every crossing where the walk enters the negative region is connected to the next crossing where it leaves it. On an ambiguous face (negative corners on a diagonal) this cuts each negative corner off separately — the inside never connects diagonally through a face.

    The rule depends only on the face's four signs. The neighbouring cell walks the same face in the opposite direction and obtains the same segments with opposite orientation, so the surface is crack-free and consistently oriented across cells by construction — the defect of tables assembled from symmetry classes and complements, which can resolve one ambiguous face differently from its two sides. Every segment has the negative region on its right, so the loops chained from the segments are wound with normals pointing from \(\phi < 0\) to \(\phi > 0\). A loop that visits one face twice is fanned from an apex with no diagonal to a crossing on a shared face, so no triangle lies inside a cell face; the tests assert that such an apex exists for every loop of all 256 cases.

    Lattice-keyed extraction¶

    MFTLExtractLevelSet places a vertex on each crossed lattice edge at the linear interpolant \(t = \phi_0/(\phi_0-\phi_1)\) and names it by its lattice edge, 3 * node + axis, computed from wrapped global node indices; its coordinates are computed from the wrapped node as well. Two ranks extracting neighbouring cell ranges therefore produce a shared vertex with the same key and bit-identical coordinates without comparing positions — the basis of the distributed reconstruction in mftl_mod_topology_change. Values closer to zero than snap * min(spacing) are pushed out to that distance (zero counts as positive), so no vertex coincides with a lattice node. A cell with an unknown (MFTL_SDF_UNKNOWN) corner is skipped.

    MFTLBuildMeshFromLevelSet extracts a whole window and builds the mesh; where the level set leaves the window the surface is open and its rim is flagged as mesh boundary, and on a periodic axis a window of exactly one period closes it across the seam.
